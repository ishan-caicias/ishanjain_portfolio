## 0. The one-sentence intent

A recruiter presses **LAUNCH** and, in about eight seconds, the daylit Earth they were parked
above falls away beneath them as the sky bleeds from blue to black and the stars come out —
landing them, gently, at the exact home vantage `goHome` would have given them, with the Where-To
console rising into place. **The ascent is the D6.4 home reveal played forward**: same sphere,
same 90° terminator geometry, same final camera pose. That identity is the spec's backbone —
post-launch and post-`goHome` must be the same frame.

## 1. Camera path — a radial climb, not a flight

The camera does **not** fly anywhere lateral. It starts close to Earth's surface on the
ra 160 / dec 0 sight-line (the home vantage bearing) and **recedes radially outward** along that
same line from a near-surface standoff to the home-orbit standoff (`ARRIVE_STANDOFF = 38`). Earth
stays pinned at the origin; the Sun direction never moves; the phase angle stays 90° the whole way
(this is why the terminator is already in frame from the first frame — it does not have to be
"found"). The felt motion is **rising away from a surface**, which is what a launch _is_, achieved
purely by the standoff growing.

- **Start standoff:** `ASCENT_START_STANDOFF = 29` world units — 3 units above the sphere surface
  (radius 26). Close enough that Earth fills the lower frame and the limb arcs overhead, per
  Astra's "pad / surface view" beat. Not _on_ the surface (26.001) — that clips the near plane and
  reads as a bug, not a launch.
- **End standoff:** `ARRIVE_STANDOFF = 38` — identical to the home-orbit radius, so the handoff is
  a zero-discontinuity match, not a cut.
- **Look-at:** the origin (Earth centre) throughout — the same look-at the home orbit uses. No
  free-look during ascent; the camera is on rails, which is correct for a title sequence.

**Altitude mapping.** The physics curve is Astra's `exp(−h/8.5 km)` in _real_ altitude 0→120 km.
The camera standoff is a _rendered_ quantity in world units. Map them so the whole 0→120 km
physical range spans the whole 29→38 standoff range, and drive the sky/star curves off the
**physical** altitude (so the colour physics stays honest) while the camera reads the **standoff**.
One eased progress parameter `k ∈ [0,1]` drives both:

```
h_km(k)      = 120 · easeAlt(k)              // physical altitude, for the sky/star/limb physics
standoff(k)  = 29 + 9 · easeCam(k)           // rendered camera distance
```

## 2. Easing — the two curves are deliberately different

A launch is not linear and it is not symmetric. Two separate eases, because the camera and the
altitude-physics want opposite shapes:

- **`easeCam(k)` — slow-out, the rocket accelerating away.** `easeInCubic`: `k³`. The camera
  barely moves for the first third (you are still _on the pad_, the world is huge and close), then
  pulls away hard. This is the single most important curve in the sequence: a linear or ease-out
  camera reads as "drifting backward", an ease-_in_ reads as "thrust". Real launches spend a long
  time low and slow, then leave fast.
- **`easeAlt(k)` — matched to the camera but slightly ahead.** `smoothstep(k)` = `k²(3−2k)`. The
  atmosphere's exponential falloff is so steep that the sky is _already_ going dark by the time the
  camera has visibly moved — which is real (sky is violet-black by 40–50 km, less than half the
  climb) and cinematically right: the colour change is the early drama, the camera speed is the
  late drama. They shouldn't peak together.

Both are pure functions of `k`, unit-tested off-GPU. `k` itself advances on **wall-clock**
(`_dtWarpS`, per the D6.4 orbit lesson — a screen-time quantity, not a physics step; the clamped
`dt` would stretch the ascent on a slow device exactly as it stretched the orbit).

## 3. The beat sheet (8.0 s nominal)

Durations are the _declared license_ — a real ascent to the Kármán line is ~8 minutes; this is
~8 seconds. The **ratios** between beats are what carry the read, so they are specified as
fractions of the total and hold at any total duration.

| Beat                    | `k` range      | `t` @ 8s   | Altitude   | What the camera/frame does                                                                                                                   | What the sky/stars do                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ----------------------- | -------------- | ---------- | ---------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **B0 Hold**             | 0.00–0.12      | 0.0–1.0 s  | 0–~4 km    | Nearly still; Earth fills lower frame, limb overhead. A held first frame reads as "ready to go" rather than "already moving".                | Full daylight sky-blue tint at horizon; no stars.                                                                                                                                                                                  |
| **B1 Ignition climb**   | 0.12–0.55      | 1.0–4.4 s  | 4–~55 km   | Camera begins pulling away, accelerating (`k³` biting now). Earth's curvature becomes visible.                                               | Sky darkens fast — blue → violet → near-black by the end of this beat (the exp curve's steep region). **First stars appear** as sky luminance crosses stellar (~50–80 km ≈ k 0.45–0.6).                                            |
| **B2 Above the line**   | 0.55–0.88      | 4.4–7.0 s  | 55–~105 km | Camera still receding but decelerating toward the standoff match. Earth reads as a _planet_ now, not a floor.                                | Full star field + Milky Way band ramped in (`uFade` 0→1 across B1–B2). **Limb ring** — the thin blue atmospheric arc — brightest here (80–120 km), then fades as it thins. This is the one honest blue-limb geometry Astra grants. |
| **B3 Settle & handoff** | 0.88–1.00      | 7.0–8.0 s  | 105–120 km | Camera eases onto the exact home-vantage pose (standoff 38, look-at origin) — a soft landing, not a stop. `cosmos:ascent-done` fires at k=1. | Sky fully black; stars/band at full; limb ring gone (the arrival geometry has no blue limb — the ring is a launch-only feature).                                                                                                   |
| **REVEAL**              | on ascent-done | +0.0–0.7 s | —          | Console rises into the 54vh dock (§5).                                                                                                       | —                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |

**No flip, no roll.** The camera holds a level horizon the entire time — the ascent's drama is
translation (rising) and colour (sky→space), not rotation. A roll here would fight the "climbing
straight up" read. The ship's own flip choreography belongs to the _warp_, not the ascent; if the
sequence later hands into a warp, the flip happens there, in D3's machinery, not here.

## 4. Continuity into the rest of the scene

The plan's line "hands off to the standard warp choreography" is satisfied by the **pose match**,
not by chaining a warp: `cosmos:ascent-done` leaves the camera at the identical
`(standoff 38, look-at origin, level horizon)` pose the idle home state uses, and the engine
transitions `warp.mode: "ascent" → "idle"` with `_homeOrbit` armed. From the visitor's point of
view the ascent _becomes_ the home orbit — the camera keeps moving, now on the slow 180 s orbit,
with no seam. The first `travelTo` after that runs the ordinary warp from the ordinary home pose.

This is the whole reason the ascent ends at the home vantage rather than anywhere dramatic: **there
is exactly one home pose in this scene, and both roads (LAUNCH and `goHome`) must arrive at it.**

## 5. The console reveal

Per the experience review, the codebase already answers the placement — the at-home dock at
`top: 54vh`. The reveal is deliberately _quiet_, because the ascent was the spectacle and the
console is the tool:

- **Trigger:** `cosmos:ascent-done` (and immediately, with no ascent, under reduced motion / the
  returning-visitor bypass).
- **Motion:** fade `opacity 0→1` **and** a `translateY(+12px → 0)` rise. Duration **0.6 s**
  (within the review's 0.5–0.7 s), easing `cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1)` — a soft
  overshoot-free settle (the same "arrival" easing family the arrival vista already uses). The
  rise is small on purpose: it says "settling into place", not "flying in".
- **Into the exact dock:** the reveal animates _to_ `top: clamp(45vh, 54vh, calc(66vh − <bar
height>))`, the formalised at-home dock — so a `goHome` later finds the console already there,
  identical. Post-launch and post-`goHome` are the same frame (§0).
- **Focus:** lands on the console **container** (`tabindex="-1"`), never the input — auto-focusing
  the input pops the mobile soft keyboard (experience review §D1.7). The container takes focus on
  reveal complete.

## 6. Reduced motion

`prefers-reduced-motion: reduce` ⇒ **instant cut**: no ascent, no camera travel, no sky
animation. LAUNCH lands the visitor directly at the home vantage with Earth revealed, stars and
band at full, and the console **already visible** in its dock (no reveal animation either). Total
wall-clock strictly ≤ the cinematic path (D1-AC6). This is not a degraded path — it is the same
destination reached without the journey, which is this repo's established reduced-motion contract
(a real position, not a default).

## 7. Skip

Skippable at **every moment** (D1.2's mandate, D1-AC3): a `SKIP ▸` affordance visible throughout
the ascent, plus Esc / Space / click. Skipping is a **jump-to-end**, not a fade — it snaps `k=1`,
fires `cosmos:ascent-done`, and reveals the console with the _reduced-motion_ (instant) reveal, so
an impatient visitor is never punished with a second animation. A recorded no-op on any input
during the ascent is a failure (D1-AC3).

## 8. Flash safety (WCAG 2.3.1 / experience review §D1.8)

The ascent stays under three luminance flashes per second. The one risk moment named by the review
is a plume relight — **there is no plume in this ascent** (RCS/thrust visuals are explicitly out of
scope this slice), so the only luminance change is the monotone sky darkening and the monotone star
ramp, neither of which flashes. The limb ring fades in and out once. No strobing surface exists.

## 9. What this spec hands to the Astra REALISM-AUDIT

The numbers Astra must check after implementation (its §4 authored them; the audit confirms the
build matches):

1. **Sky-colour curve** — is the blue→black transition keyed to `exp(−h/8.5)` in _physical_
   altitude, not to the eased camera standoff or to `k` directly?
2. **Star-reveal altitude** — do the first stars cross in at ~50–80 km (k ≈ 0.45–0.6), not earlier
   (which would read as "already in space") or later?
3. **Limb-ring geometry** — is the blue arc present only in 80–120 km and gone by the handoff, so
   the arrival geometry (which has no honest blue limb) never inherits it?
4. **Carried-forward from D6.4** — the ascent renders Earth at α = 90° throughout, the same
   geometry the D6.4 realism review flagged (`PLANET_LUNAR_L.earth = 0`, albedo 0.213 both fitted
   at α = 0). D1.3 does not make this worse, but it is the second feature to ship on that geometry,
   so the audit should state whether it now warrants Astra's re-derivation before more is built on
   it.
