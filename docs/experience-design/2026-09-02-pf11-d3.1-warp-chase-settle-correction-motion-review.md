## Why 4.3, re-derived from the brief's own rationale

The SHOT-BRIEF's §3 "Why these numbers" is explicit about what the settle value is _for_: after
the 5.3→5.8 braking pull-back, the camera "**eases into the arrival framing**" over
k 0.72→1.0, landing on 4.3 at k≈0.9 specifically so the close-in is _gradual_ — "the settle reads
as arriving, ... not as acceleration" — and `smoothstep` puts the fastest closing rate near
k≈0.85, chosen to compose with the destination's own closure-rate ease-out rather than fight it.
That is a claim about the **shape of the close-in curve**, not merely its endpoints. At 3.4 the
curve does something the brief never asked for: **2.4 of the total 2.8-unit close-in (5.8→3.0)
happens inside k 0.72→0.92** (a 0.20-wide window), leaving only 0.4 units for the last 0.08 of
k — i.e. most of the "settle" is actually a second, steeper push-in compressed right after the
pull-back, with a comparatively flat tail. At 4.3 the close-in is 1.5 units over 0.72→0.92 and
1.3 units over 0.92→1.0 — genuinely graded across the full tail, which is what "monotone-settle"
was describing. This is the concrete version of the owner's real-device report: a camera that
closes in _faster and sooner_ than the spec's own curve reads as the ship being pulled toward
the camera again, not as an unhurried arrival — "shrinking away" in reverse, still a
CONTRADICTS-adjacent read, just relocated to the tail instead of the mid-brake.

## Why the k-shift did not require the back-distance shift (the actual gap)

TR-093's recorded deviation (§"Deviation recorded", lines 123-128) and the 2026-07-24 MOTION-AUDIT
both justify _only_ moving k from 0.9 to 0.92, to keep the pre-existing 30°-elevation-hold unit
test (`tests/unit/ship-dynamics.test.ts`, asserts `-u/b = tan(30°)` for k ∈ [0.1, 0.92]) passing
without shrinking its window. That justification is correct and is **kept** — k=0.92 is
unchanged by this fix.

What neither doc explains is why _back_ also moved, from 4.3 to 3.4. It didn't need to: `elev(b)
= -tan(30°)·b` is linear in `b`, so for two waypoints that both satisfy `up = elev(back)`,
interpolating `up` and `back` with the identical smoothstep weight `s` gives
`up(s) = elev(back_i) + s·(elev(back_j) - elev(back_i)) = elev(back_i + s·(back_j - back_i)) =
elev(back(s))` — the interpolated point sits exactly on the elevation locus **for any back
values at the endpoints**, not only for 3.4. The 30°-hold invariant is a statement about `k`
alone (which waypoints' `k`s bracket a given sample) and is indifferent to what `back` is at
those waypoints, provided `up = elev(back)` at each — which the code always computes. So the
back-distance change was never load-bearing for the test that TR-093 cited to justify it: it was
carried along with the k-shift, unexamined, and "zero perceptual cost" was asserted rather than
measured (exactly as the triage doc names it). This audit's verdict on the earlier "READS" call:
the k-shift half was correctly reasoned; the back-distance half was not reasoned at all, and, per
the analysis above, does not read the same. **Correcting the deviation record, not erasing it**
— TR-093 and the 2026-07-24 MOTION-AUDIT stand as written; this is the superseding assessment.
