## 1. What this beat must communicate

**"We have turned the ship around and we are now slowing down."** The owner's complaint (R6)
is that the decel doesn't read as slowing — the ship reaches the flip, then the destination
arrives without the body ever feeling the brakes. Astra §3.2 is explicit: perceived braking is
carried ~80% by two cues — **closure-rate ease-out on the destination's angular size** and the
**plume/attitude reversal** — and every speed cue must derive from `d(warpEase)/dk`, never from
`k`. D3.1 owns the _closure_ half of that (the chase framing and the lens); D3.2 owns the flip
and plume reversal.

The single organising rule for this brief: **during braking, nothing in the frame may grow
toward the camera faster than it did while cruising.** A ship that looms larger while "slowing"
is the textbook CONTRADICTS defect, and it is exactly what the current chase table does.

## 2. The defect, in one line

`CHASE_WAYPOINTS` closes the camera from back-distance **5.3 → 3.5 → 2.2** across k 0.55→0.92,
so the ship grows continuously through the whole braking phase and is at its **largest** (back
2.2, nearer than the arrival framing of 3.0) at k=0.92 — reading as _accelerating into us_ at
the moment it should read as braking. The velocity profile is correct; the camera is telling
the opposite story.

## 3. The fix — chase back-distance opens, then settles (never looms)

Rework only the post-flip waypoints (k > 0.55); the accel/flip framing (k ≤ 0.55) is not the
complaint and stays. `chaseOffsetAt` is driven by **raw k**, interpolated with per-segment
`smoothstep` (zero slope at every waypoint) — so continuity is structural: **C¹ with zero
velocity at every waypoint boundary and at k=1**, no pops, provided the values are monotone
where they need to be.

**Replacement `CHASE_WAYPOINTS` (the two post-flip rows change; a third is added):**

| k        | right    | back    | up (= −tan30°·back) | Beat                                                                                   |
| -------- | -------- | ------- | ------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 0.0      | 0        | 3.0     | 0                   | astern start (invariant)                                                               |
| 0.1      | 0        | 2.6     | elev(2.6)           | (unchanged)                                                                            |
| 0.35     | 0        | 4.5     | elev(4.5)           | pan out on the accel burn (unchanged)                                                  |
| 0.55     | 0.6      | 5.3     | elev(5.3)           | flip peak (unchanged)                                                                  |
| **0.72** | **0.25** | **5.8** | **elev(5.8)**       | **NEW — braking pull-back: camera eases _back_, ship recedes ~9%**                     |
| **0.9**  | **0**    | **4.3** | **elev(4.3)**       | **NEW — settling begins, elevation held ~30°, lateral gone**                           |
| 1.0      | 0        | 3.0     | 0                   | arrival framing, level (invariant — assert `chaseOffsetAt(1)===[0,0,SHIP_VIEW_DEPTH]`) |

**Why these numbers.**

- **5.3 → 5.8 (open, k 0.55→0.72):** just after the flip, the camera drifts _back_ half a world
  unit (~9% further), so the ship's apparent size _decreases_ right when the retro-burn lights.
  This is the follow-through on the flip and the first physical "we're braking" beat — the body
  reads a pull-back, not a push-in. Kept gentle (a bigger open would read as the ship _shrinking
  away_, a different and wrong story).
- **5.8 → 4.3 → 3.0 (monotone settle, k 0.72→1.0):** the camera eases into the arrival framing.
  Crucially the back-distance **never drops below 3.0 before k=1**, so the ship never exceeds its
  arrival size during braking — the settle reads as _arriving_, the follow-through into the dock,
  not as acceleration. `smoothstep` puts the fastest closing near k≈0.85 where `dsdk`≈0.6 is
  already low, so it composes with — rather than fights — the destination's ease-out.
- **Elevation** holds ~30° (the behind-and-above hero convention) through k=0.9, then de-elevates
  to level over the last 10% for the straight-astern arrival framing — same convention as today,
  just reached from a wider standoff.
- **Lateral** decays 0.6 → 0.25 → 0: the flip's depth-cue offset resolves to dead-astern by
  arrival (unchanged intent).

**Composition with Astra §3.2's closure-rate ease-out.** The destination's angular growth is
driven by the ship _position_ (`warpEase`), whose closure rate `dsdk = 4(1−k)` already eases to
0 at k=1 — that half is physics and is correct. The chase-open is decoupled from it: ±0.5 world
units of standoff is negligible against interstellar `D_total`, so opening the chase changes the
**ship's** apparent size without meaningfully touching the **destination's**. The two cues
therefore reinforce (destination grows with decreasing rate; ship holds/recedes then settles)
instead of competing. This is the whole point of driving both from the derivative.

## 4. The fix — FOV breathing (the missing speed cue)

Port the legacy lens breath, driven by the same `dsdk` triangle every other cue uses so they
cannot disagree:

```
fovMult(k) = 1 + FOV_GAIN * dsdk / 2        // dsdk = k<0.5 ? 4k : 4(1−k)
camera.fov = clamp(baseFov * fovMult, baseFov, baseFov * 1.08)
FOV_GAIN   = 0.06
```

- `dsdk` peaks at **2** at k=0.5, so `fovMult` peaks at **1.06** — the lens widens **+6%** at
  peak speed and relaxes to base at both ends. Widening reads _faster_ (more peripheral streak
  flow); the relaxation across k 0.5→1.0 is a continuous, monotone **"slowing" cue** that runs
  the entire braking phase. At k=0.5 the widen-rate reverses to a narrow-rate — a C⁰ kink in the
  lens, but it lands exactly on the turnaround and is shared with β and the streaks (which carry
  the identical `dsdk` kink and ship fine today), so it reads as _the flip_, not a glitch. No
  extra smoothing — cue-agreement is the feature.
- **The base FOV is the camera's runtime FOV, NOT `SHIP_BASE_FOV`.** ⚠ Integration trap Procyon
  must honour: the `FreeCamera` runs at Babylon's default `fov = 0.8 rad` (≈45.8°); `SHIP_BASE_FOV`
  (70°) is used _only_ for ship-mesh scale math and is never assigned to the camera. Capture
  `this._baseFov = camera.fov` once at construction and breathe around that. Using `SHIP_BASE_FOV`
  would snap the lens 45.8°→70° on warp start — a violent pop. At the true base, +6% is +2.75°
  (0.8→0.848 rad): present but subliminal, which is right for a lens cue.
- **Reset to base off the burn.** FOV breathing applies **only in warp mode**. The arrival branch
  (`k>=1`), the idle/aim paths, and the ascent path must all leave `camera.fov = _baseFov`. At k=1
  `dsdk=0` so the breath is already at base — value-continuous into idle, no pop.

## 6. HUD label (a label is a cue — D3.1 owns it)

`WarpOverlay.tsx` currently prints **"DECELERATION BURN"** for the decel phase. Rename to
**"BRAKING BURN"** — shorter, active, and it matches the NASA-ops voice register the rest of the
HUD uses (`ACCELERATION BURN` / `FLIP & BURN` / `BRAKING BURN`). A label that says "deceleration"
over a picture that finally _shows_ braking is fine, but "BRAKING BURN" is the tighter cue and is
the string the cinematics map already standardises on.

**Seam note for D3.2 (do now, cheap):** the `wphase` thresholds are hard-coded literals
(`k < 0.47 ? "accel" : k < 0.53 ? "flip" : "decel"`, babylon-engine ~6027) while `flipPhase`
reads the exported `WARP_ACCEL_END`/`WARP_DECEL_START` constants. Source the `wphase` computation
from those same constants so D3.2's window-widening (0.47→0.44, 0.53→0.56) is a one-place change
and the label boundaries can never drift from the hull choreography.

## 7. Reduced motion & tiers

- **Reduced motion:** unaffected by §3 — reduced journeys use `CHASE_OFFSET_REST` (constant
  `[0,0,3]`) and never call `chaseOffsetAt`, so the waypoint changes are inert there. FOV breathing
  is **off** (`FOV_GAIN` effectively 0 → `camera.fov = _baseFov`). The information (we arrived) is
  preserved by the existing instant-swap. ✓
- **Tiers:** FOV breathing is one clamped scalar write per warp frame — no allocation, no pass,
  free on every tier including the WebGL2 fallback. It ships everywhere; only reduced-motion
  disables it. The chase-table change is pure data, identical on all tiers.

## 8. Frame budget & envelope

- **Budget:** the chase change is data-only; FOV is one `Math.min/max` + assignment per warp
  frame. Negligible, no per-frame GC. (Procyon confirms in the gate.)
- **Astra envelope / ledger:** FOV breathing is a _lens/framing_ choice, not a claim about the
  sky's geometry — it falls under the standing "cinematic flight-feel" license that already covers
  the damped-spring chase camera, and the widen _reinforces_ the real aberration rather than
  contradicting it. **No new ledger entry expected**; flagged for Astra to confirm (or ledger) in
  the post-D3 REALISM-AUDIT. The chase-open is likewise within the existing flight-feel license.

## 9. Owner-eyes checklist (what the preview must show — E2E can't judge feel)

Travel to one near body (Mars) and one far (M42), watch the **second half** of each journey:

1. **The ship does not loom.** Through braking the ship holds or gently recedes, then eases to
   its arrival size — it is never larger mid-brake than at the dock. (The old build's ship was
   biggest just before arrival.)
2. **The lens relaxes.** A subtle narrowing of the field across the braking half — peripheral
   streaks should feel like they're _settling_, not holding constant.
3. **The pull-back beat reads.** Just after the flip, a gentle sense of the camera easing back
   off the ship as the retro-burn takes hold.
4. **No pop** at the flip→brake handoff, at k≈0.72/0.9, or at arrival — the settle into the dock
   is smooth and slow, not a final lurch.
5. **Label matches picture:** "BRAKING BURN" is showing while the above happens.
6. **Reduced motion** still arrives instantly and correctly, with no lens change.

## 10. Curve & constant appendix (ready to implement)

```
// ship-dynamics.ts — CHASE_WAYPOINTS, replace the two post-flip rows [0.8,…],[0.92,…] with:
[0.72, 0.25, elev(5.8), 5.8],
[0.9,  0,    elev(4.3), 4.3],
// (rows for k ≤ 0.55 and the k=1 invariant row unchanged)

// babylon-engine.ts — capture once at camera construction:
this._baseFov = camera.fov;                    // Babylon default 0.8 rad; NOT SHIP_BASE_FOV

// FOV_GAIN as a named constant (ship-dynamics.ts, next to the chase constants):
export const WARP_FOV_GAIN = 0.06;
export const WARP_FOV_MAX_MULT = 1.08;         // safety clamp

// per warp frame in _tickWarp (non-reduced, mode === "warp"), with dsdk already computed:
const mult = Math.min(1 + WARP_FOV_GAIN * dsdk / 2, WARP_FOV_MAX_MULT);
camera.fov = this._baseFov * mult;
// on k>=1 arrival, and in reduced / non-warp / ascent paths:
camera.fov = this._baseFov;
```

**Continuity classes:** chase offset C¹ (smoothstep per segment, zero-velocity joints); FOV C⁰
at k=0.5 (shared, intentional turnaround kink), value-continuous into idle at k=1. Both
reduced-motion variants are constant (no motion).
