## 1. The placement is the finding, and it is now implemented as specified

Astra §B3's verdict was that Earth as a catalog entry with any ra/dec is **BROKEN PHYSICS**: the
catalog is a geocentric apparent-place catalog, so Earth's direction is 0/0 and its distance is 0 —
and `bodyDepth`'s `(ly || 0.001)` fallback would place a true zero at depth 172.577, _deeper than
Neptune_. The scheme cannot express "here."

The shipped code does not attempt to. Earth has no catalog entry, `travelTo("earth")` is a verified
no-op, and the sphere is driven by a second, body-less path keyed on being at the home vantage.
**This is the recommended option implemented as recommended**, and it is worth noting that it was
the _more_ honest option rather than a compromise: it unlocks four features for the one body that
has them at zero cost to the other twelve.

## 2. The 90° vantage: verified, not inherited

The brief computes the vantage as `u = (−0.939693, 0.342020, 0)`, camera at
`38·u = (−35.708, 12.997, 0.000)`, and `u · sunDir = −3.3 × 10⁻¹⁶`.

This audit re-derived it independently from the two catalog data (Sun ra 250 / dec −20.5; vantage
ra 160 / dec 0) and confirms **90.0000°** to four decimal places, with the shipped
`homeOrbitPosition(0)` landing at (−35.708, 12.997, 0.000) to 2 dp. A unit test recomputes it
rather than restating it, so if either the Sun's entry or the parked bearing moves, the claim
fails loudly.

**The invariance is the part that needed checking beyond the brief.** The brief specifies a
vantage; R16 asks for an _orbit_, which the brief did not analyse. Orbiting about an arbitrary axis
would sweep the phase angle through the full 0-180° range and destroy the very geometry the vantage
was chosen for. The shipped orbit takes the **Sun direction as its axis**, which makes the phase
angle a conserved quantity by construction — every point of the circle is 90° from the Sun. Checked
at 24 points around the revolution and live in E2E at two separated samples. **This is correct and
it is the only axis choice that is.**

## 3. The night map is not decoded, and that was measured

The linear-pipeline rule from D6.3.1 says colour textures are decoded before being multiplied. The
night map is not, and the reasoning is worth recording because "it's a colour, decode it" is the
plausible wrong answer:

| Space  | mean   | p95    | p99    | p999      | max   |
| ------ | ------ | ------ | ------ | --------- | ----- |
| raw    | 0.0513 | 0.0925 | 0.0986 | 0.2599    | 0.792 |
| linear | 0.0045 | 0.0089 | 0.0098 | **0.055** | 0.590 |

The composite subtracts a floor of 0.06 before applying the gain. **In linear space that floor sits
above the 99.9th percentile of the map** — it would erase essentially everything and leave a nearly
black night side. The floor/gain pair is a _declared emissive composite_ calibrated against the real
radiance ratio in the space the Black Marble product was authored in; it is not a reflectance being
lit, so the rule that governs `surface` does not govern it. Same category as the cloud map, whose
raw byte Astra had already validated for the same kind of reason.

**Verdict ACCURATE**, with the caveat that this is a calibration statement rather than a physical
one — which is exactly what §6 of the brief calls it.

## 4. The masking is right, and it is the thing that mattered

Astra ranked "showing city lights at all at α = 0 — compositing the night map unconditionally, or
masking on anything other than the true N·L" as **broken physics #1**, and named the temptation
explicitly: _"It is the single most beautiful thing in the asset pack... Nobody checks the phase
angle."_

Shipped: `lit += night * NIGHT_GAIN * (1.0 - dayside) * (1.0 - cloudA)`. `dayside` is the exact
`smoothstep` term that lights the day side, so `1 - dayside` is its exact complement — a city can
appear only where the Sun genuinely cannot reach, and the real solar-penumbra softening
(`TERMINATOR_SOFTEN`) carries over to the light/dark transition for free. Cloud attenuation is
additionally correct: clouds occlude cities.

Unit-asserted in **both twins**, and the assertion is on the mask specifically, not merely on the
term's presence.

## 5. The declared trade, restated so it is not forgotten

At this vantage the **ocean glint is lost** — its specular point sits 45° off-frame. Glint and city
lights are mutually exclusive at this 45.84° FOV; the brief quantified this and D6.4 takes the
trade knowingly. What is gained: terminator at frame centre, night hemisphere ~50% of frame, the
~290 px twilight band, cloud shadows at 12-25 px instead of 0.45 px, and the city lights.

**Accepted**, and it is worth noting the trade is taken _for Earth alone_ — every other body keeps
its glint-favourable zero-phase arrival untouched.

## 6. ⚠ The one open item, carried forward

**`PLANET_LUNAR_L.earth = 0` and `PLANET_ALBEDO.earth = 0.213` were both fitted at α = 0, and D6.4
changes α to 90°.**

The Lunar-Lambert coefficient's own source comment says this plainly: the choice "changes the
render by 0% at frame centre and at most 8.6% at the frame edge" **at the zero-phase arrival**, and
"becomes a 37-67% error the moment the arrival phase ever changes." That moment has now arrived.

This is not a defect introduced by D6.4 — the constants are unchanged and were correct for the
geometry they were fitted at. But the audited build renders Earth at a geometry where the brief
itself predicts a large error, and the honest position is that **Earth's photometry has not been
re-derived for α = 90° and should be before D1.3 builds the launch ascent on this same geometry.**

Recommended follow-up (not blocking this slice, but named):

1. Re-derive the surface's effective coefficient at 90° phase for a water/land composite.
2. Re-check the 0.213 clear-sky albedo split, which was closed against a _disc-integrated_ budget
   at zero phase; at quadrature the visible hemisphere is half terminator-adjacent.
3. Both want Astra's numbers rather than a renderer's guess.

## 8. What this audit did NOT cover

- **Whether the city lights are legible on real hardware.** Every automated check confirms the term
  is present, correctly masked, correctly ordered relative to the tone map, and that Earth renders
  with a real terminator. Whether the cities _read_ at this scale, on a real GPU, at the `full`
  tier, is owner-eyes work — and depends on which longitude faces the camera, since Earth rotates
  once per ~86 s of screen time.
- **The twilight band.** The brief predicts ~290 px at this geometry; nothing here measures it.
- **§6's photometry.** Explicitly deferred above rather than silently assumed fine.
