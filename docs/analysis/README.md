- [2026-07-21-planetary-sphere-topography-science-brief.md](2026-07-21-planetary-sphere-topography-science-brief.md) — **Astra science brief for PF-10 C4's real planetary spheres with real topography** (Astra, 2026-07-21); replaces the flat billboard quads (267 bodies sharing a 4096² atlas at 128² per cell) with sphere meshes textured from the local equirectangular imagery and the MOLA/LOLA elevation pyramids. Headline finding: **true-scale relief is invisible on the silhouette** — Mars must render 2260 px across before its topography bends the limb by one pixel (the Moon, 695 px) — but true-scale _shading_ is fully visible because slope is scale-free, so the recommendation is **exaggeration 1.0** and never to scale the normal map. Four BROKEN PHYSICS findings: (a) Gaia Sky's own `heightScale` for the Moon (5.5 km vs real 19.9 km) and Mercury (1.2848 km vs real 9.9 km) render them **3.6× and 7.7× too flat** — Mars (21.287 km) and Earth (8.848 km = Everest, land-only, verified by a measured median byte of 0) are near-physical; (b) Lambertian shading is wrong for the Moon — real regolith backscatter makes the full Moon a _flat evenly-lit disc_, not a limb-darkened ball, and the half Moon only ~9% as bright as full, fixed by the one-divide Lunar-Lambert law (`L=1.0` Moon, `L≈0.55` Mars); (c) sharing the belt's `TIME_ACCEL = 4×10⁵` would spin Mars at **4.5 rev/s = 27.1°/frame**, past Nyquist and aliasing backwards — recommends a separate `ROTATION_TIME_ACCEL = 1e3` (Mars turns in 89 s, all real period ratios preserved); (d) sphering Phobos/Deimos, the most famously irregular bodies available. Verified from the files that the **MOLA 64K virtual texture is also 8-bit JPEG** — it buys spatial resolution only, pushing the minimum representable slope from 1.84° to **14.41°** (a measured level5 tile spans just 14 byte values across 1024²), so the pyramid is good for large landforms and useless for fine normals; mitigation is an offline wide-baseline dithered normal bake. Confirms a `DirectionalLight` is ACCURATE (Sun subtends 0.350° at Mars, terminator 3.1 px on a 1000-px disc) and corrects the requested albedos — 0.25/0.12 are **Bond**, the renderer wants **geometric** 0.170 (Mars) and 0.136 (Moon). Corrects the asset inventory (56 base files not 105; callisto, enceladus, uranus and neptune textures **absent** despite being referenced; Mars ships no normal map) and locates the Mars height-map extrema at Hellas and Tharsis, fixing the texture origin at lon 180°E. Names the honest unlock: **real phases and a real terminator** — billboards are permanently "full", while a lit sphere with real elevation casts 172 km (50 px) crater shadows at grazing light
- [2026-07-21-venus-cloud-descent-science-brief.md](2026-07-21-venus-cloud-descent-science-brief.md) — **Astra science brief for PF-10 C4.2's Venus cloud-descent arrival** (Astra, 2026-07-21); the owner's decision (TR-076 Part 7) to fly the arrival _through_ the real cloud deck rather than present a Magellan radar map as a view. Supplies the descent's keyframes as **real altitudes** — cloud top 70 km (τ=1), upper/middle boundary 56.5 km, the **55 km "habitable layer" at 0.53 bar and +30 °C**, middle/lower boundary 50.5 km, **cloud base 47.5 km (the breakout, and the real payoff — not 0 km)**, haze base 31 km, supercritical CO₂ below ~8 km — with VIRA pressure/temperature and a measured illuminance curve at each. Two **measured** asset findings that change the design: **`venus-cloud.jpg` is a UV map, not a visible-light one** (R=G=B single band, σ/mean = **20.5 %** against real visible cloud contrast of 1–3 %), and **`venus-ultra.jpg`'s orange tint (1.00, 0.57, 0.20) is the real Venera surface illuminant**, derived independently here from Rayleigh transmission. Derives the CO₂ Rayleigh optical depth of the whole atmosphere at **τ(550 nm) = 18.7 — 190× Earth's** — which fixes why the light below is orange (blue dies at 43.5 km, green 37.1 km, red 30.4 km) and caps surface visibility at **3.3 km**. **The crux (§3): presenting the radar map is fine as a declared instrument view, but _lighting_ it is BROKEN PHYSICS** — a "plausible visible-light interpretation" is unavailable because radar brightness is roughness, not reflectance, and every Venera site found near-uniform basalt at albedo 0.10, so any interpretation would invent contrast the planet does not have. Mandates a **body-specific shading fork**: no directional light, no terminator, **`venus-normal.jpg` never bound** (it converts backscatter into fake relief), no C4.3 self-shadow — which is also the physically correct answer, since Venera recorded flat shadowless orange light with no solar disc. Quantifies super-rotation (**100 m/s at cloud top, 4.45 d lap, 54.6× the 243 d retrograde solid body, 0.3–1.0 m/s at the surface**) and rules that descent-time cloud advection must run at **real time 1.0×** — `ROTATION_TIME_ACCEL = 1e3` would make cloud-top wind **100 km/s = 0.033 c** — while the cloud _shell's_ global rotation may use 1e3, preserving the 54.6× ratio exactly. Recommends a **20 s descent with 50 % of it inside the deck**, and **not landing** (stop at 15–25 km; at 0 km you can see 3.3 km and nothing else). Flags four pushbacks: lighting the radar map is the largest undetectable dishonesty risk; a dramatic swirling deck is the same error inverted (ship a labelled UV mode instead); the descent must not be generalised to bodies with no surface; and Venus lightning is genuinely **contested** (Akatsuki optical null vs VLF detections) and should be omitted. Notes atmospheric refraction is real and spectacular (**ray curvature radius 1264 km vs the planet's 6052 km** — you would stand in a bowl) but **unobservable**, cancelled by the 3.3 km visibility. **Supersedes** the C4.1 brief's §6 "render the bland cloud deck" recommendation, with the reversal recorded rather than edited away

- ~~The checked-in Astro app does not yet contain the traveling spaceship scene; that logic still lives in the prototype folder.~~ (void — see audit)
- The prototype's ship feels partly 2D because the scene mixes a real 3D star renderer with several fixed-screen DOM and clip-space presentation layers.
- The safest replacement path is not "find the most animated Sketchfab file"; it is "pick a legally clean, performance-safe ship with a strong silhouette, then decide whether animation stays procedural or moves to glTF."
- Language and framework upgrades alone will not fix the realism gap. The meaningful unlock is glTF-friendly rendering, not a general Astro or React migration.
