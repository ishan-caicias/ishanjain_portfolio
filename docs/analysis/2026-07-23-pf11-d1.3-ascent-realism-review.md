## 1. The sky-colour curve is genuinely barometric

The sky background lerps `mix(space-black, day-blue, exp(−h/8.5))`. Two things make this honest
rather than a hand-drawn fade, and both are unit-asserted:

- It **e-folds by exactly 1/e per 8.5 km scale height** (`skyMix(H)` = e⁻¹, `skyMix(2H)/skyMix(H)`
  = e⁻¹). That is the barometric law, not a curve that merely looks like one.
- It is keyed to the **physical altitude** `120·easeAlt(k)`, not to the eased camera standoff or to
  `k` directly. This matters: the sky is near-black by 40 km (`skyMix(40)` < 0.01), which is under
  half the climb — so the colour leads the camera, exactly as it does in real ascent footage where
  the sky goes dark long before the vehicle is "in space". Keying it to the camera would have made
  the sky darken linearly with the visible motion, which is wrong and reads wrong.

**Verdict ACCURATE.** The one judgement call an audit should flag: `DAY_SKY_RGB = (0.3, 0.5, 0.8)`
is a chosen daylight tint, not a measured radiance. It is a reasonable mid-daylight horizon blue
and the curve that consumes it is correct; if the owner wants the pad sky to match a specific
real-sky luminance that is a tuning input, not a physics correction.

## 2. The star reveal is at the right altitude

`starFade = smoothstep(50, 80, h)` — zero below 50 km, full above 80 km. This matches Astra's "first
stars appear as sky luminance drops below stellar (~50–80 km)". The mechanism is honest too: the
star field is additively blended, so it is **naturally** washed out against the bright pad sky and
pops as the background darkens — the reveal emerges from the sky curve rather than being faked by a
separate star-brightness ramp. The band's own `uFade` is driven by the same `starFade` so the band
and stars come up together. **ACCURATE.**

## 3. The limb ring — right window, simplified form, and that is declared

The ring is visible only in 80–120 km (`limbIntensity`, a single smooth bump, unit-asserted
monotone up-then-down so it can never strobe) and **zero at the handoff altitude**, so the arrival
geometry — which has no honest blue limb — never inherits it. That geometry gating is the honest
core of the beat and it is correct.

The **form** is a simplification, and it should be named as one: the ring is a fresnel-rim shell
just outside Earth (alpha ∝ `(1 − |N·V|)³`), not a physically modelled atmospheric scattering
integral. A real limb is the sight-line integral of Rayleigh scattering through a growing air
column toward the tangent point; the fresnel rim reproduces its _appearance_ (a bright arc hugging
the silhouette, brightest at the edge, fading inward) without the integral. It is additionally
gated by `N·sun` so only the **sunlit** limb glows — a night-side limb glow would be scattered
light with no incident sunlight to scatter, the same honesty rule the D6.4 night-lights mask
follows. **Verdict: ACCURATE geometry, SIMPLIFIED form, declared (ledger L10).**

## 4. The camera path is the honest one

A launch-from-Earth in a geocentric frame is a **radial** recession from the origin — "straight up"
from the surface _is_ "outward from Earth's centre". The camera climbs the ra 160 / dec 0
sight-line (the home vantage bearing) from standoff 29 to 38, look-at origin, level horizon, no
roll. This is not a stylistic choice dressed as physics: a vertical ascent really does hold that
line, and holding the horizon level is correct for a title sequence where the drama is translation
(rising), not rotation. The phase angle stays 90° throughout because neither the Sun nor the
sight-line moves — which is why the terminator is in frame from the first frame rather than being
"found". **ACCURATE.**

## 5. The pose-match handoff

The single most load-bearing number: the ascent's end standoff is **38 = `ARRIVE_STANDOFF` =
`HOME_ORBIT_RADIUS`**, unit-asserted equal. So `cosmos:ascent-done` leaves the camera at the exact
home pose, and the engine transitions `ascent → idle` with the home orbit armed at phase 0. LAUNCH
and `goHome` arrive at the identical frame — the ascent is the reveal played forward, not a
separate cinematic that happens to end near home. **ACCURATE**, and it is the thing that makes the
whole "same sphere, both roads" claim true rather than approximate.

## 6. ⚠ ESCALATED: Earth's α = 90° photometry, now shipped twice

The D6.4 realism review flagged that `PLANET_LUNAR_L.earth = 0` and `PLANET_ALBEDO.earth = 0.213`
were both fitted at α = 0, that D6.4 renders Earth at α = 90°, and that the Earth brief's own source
comment predicts this is a **37–67 % error** at the frame edge. It recommended closing it **"before
D1.3 builds the ascent on this same geometry."**

D1.3 has now built the ascent on that geometry without the photometry being re-derived. This is not
a regression D1.3 introduced — the constants are unchanged and D1.3 does not make the error worse —
but the audit will not quietly re-defer a second time. **The recommendation is escalated:**

- The item now blocks _nothing already shipped_ (both features work and are console-clean), but it
  **should block the next Earth-visual change** — and the ascent's own polish (does the terminator
  read at the right softness? is the twilight band the predicted ~290 px?) cannot be judged until
  the surface photometry under it is correct at α = 90°.
- Concretely wanted from Astra, unchanged from the D6.4 review: (1) the surface's effective
  Lunar-Lambert coefficient at 90° phase for a water/land composite; (2) a re-check of the 0.213
  clear-sky split, closed at α = 0 against a disc-integrated budget, at a geometry where the visible
  hemisphere is half terminator-adjacent.

This is the honest state: the ascent is correct in everything it _added_, and sits on top of one
inherited constant that two features now depend on and that the science says is wrong at this angle.

## 8. What this audit did NOT cover

- **Whether the ascent reads well on real hardware.** Every physics claim here is computed or unit-
  asserted; the SwiftShader E2E confirms it runs end-to-end console-clean and lands at 90° phase,
  but the actual look of the blue→black transition, the star reveal timing _as felt_, and the limb
  arc need the owner's eyes on a real GPU at the `full` tier.
- **The twilight band** (~290 px predicted at this geometry) — unmeasured, and §6's photometry gates
  a fair judgement of it anyway.
- **The two cheapest UX validations** the delivery plan's pre-build gate names (LAUNCH-gate hallway
  test ×5, dossier-vs-spinner probe) are human studies, out of scope for a realism audit — flagged
  in the TR as owner-run.

## Addendum (2026-07-24 — the §6 escalation is CLOSED)

The escalated α = 90° photometry item has been computed and fixed — see the
[Earth brief's Addendum 3](2026-07-21-earth-sphere-science-brief.md#addendum-3-2026-07-24-astra--procyon--the-α--90-photometry-computed-and-closed)
for the derivation and TR-092 for the gate. Both concrete asks were answered: (1) the surface's
effective Lunar-Lambert coefficient at 90° is **still 0** (the no-regolith mechanism is
phase-independent; residual surface anisotropy at 90° is a few percent); (2) **0.213 stands**
(its α = 0 anchor was corroborated independently and is not the deficit's source). The real gap
was the cloud slab's missing Mie forward-scattering deficit — closed with the solved
`CLOUD_QUAD_DEFICIT = 0.539` (Φ_shader(90°) now = 0.236, measured-exact; ledger L18). The
ascent's own polish (terminator softness, twilight band) can now be judged on correct surface
photometry, per §6's condition. This closes the last open item from the D1.3 slice.
