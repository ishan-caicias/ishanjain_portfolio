## 1. The surge relocation removes a genuine broken-physics finding

The prior state applied a geometric albedo **uniformly across the whole disc**. Geometric albedo
is defined at phase angle 0.000° and is only valid there; Verbiscer et al. measured Tethys's 1.229
precisely _by observing at sub-0.1° phase, because that is the only place the peak exists_. Painting
it disc-wide is a real, correctly-measured number applied at a geometry where it does not hold —
the same failure shape as lighting a radar map (TR-078), and the same taxonomy entry: **domain
error, nothing fabricated**.

The shipped construction is Astra's own:

```
base  = A_B                                  (Bond albedo, energy-bounded)
surge = 1 + B0 · exp(−α_deg / 2.0°)
B0    = p/A_B − 1                            ⇒  base · surge(0) ≡ p
```

Verified numerically in `planet-sphere.test.ts` for all four bodies: Tethys 0.80 × 1.536 = 1.2288
(published 1.229), Dione 0.63 × 1.584 = 0.9979 (0.998), Rhea 0.48 × 1.977 = 0.9490 (0.949), Moon
0.11 × 1.236 = 0.1360 (0.136). **The change is exactly lossless at the geometry where the published
number applies, and correct — rather than merely dimmer — everywhere else.**

This scene arrives at α = 0.000° for every body (the arrival geometry pins V = L), which is the one
and only geometry at which an opposition surge is visible at all. The bodies carrying the strongest
surges ever measured are therefore rendered at the exact camera that can show them. That was true
before this slice too — but before, the surge was a flat multiplier with no angular structure. It
now has the real 2°-scale bloom.

## 2. The sRGB decode, and why the exclusions are the substantive part

Decoding `surfaceTex` is straightforward CLAUDE.md #10 compliance. The audit-relevant decision is
what was **not** decoded, and each exclusion is correct for a distinct reason:

| Sampler                  | Left raw | Why that is right                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| ------------------------ | -------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| `cloudTex`               | ✅       | A coverage **fraction**, not a colour. Astra had already validated the RAW byte against Earth's real geometric albedo (7% closure vs 33% if sRGB-decoded). Decoding it would re-break a term the Earth brief specifically got right. |
| `specularTex`            | ✅       | An ocean/land **mask**.                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| `heightTex`              | ✅       | **Elevation** data, differentiated into slopes. A transfer curve here would non-linearly distort terrain gradients.                                                                                                                  |
| `normalTex`, `detailTex` | ✅       | **Vectors** in [−1,1] after decode-by-2×−1. Applying a photometric transfer function to a direction is meaningless.                                                                                                                  |

A single blanket "decode every texture" would have silently corrupted four of the five. This is the
class of change where being thorough is worse than being precise.

## 3. The gain is derived, and the derivation is auditable

`EXPOSURE_GAIN = 1.9` is not a taste value and should not be read as one. The instrument
re-measured the cos-latitude-weighted mean luminance of every shipped surface map and **reproduced
Astra's independent A2.3 table to three decimals** on the nine bodies both measured — an instrument
validity check of exactly the kind CLAUDE.md's measurement discipline asks for, passed before any
number derived from it was trusted.

It then found something A2.3 did not report: **eight bodies clip today, not six.** Saturn (1.500)
and Jupiter (1.207) were never in the brief's table, which sampled nine of the sixteen shipped
bodies. That is an additive correction to A2.3, not a contradiction of it.

The gain was then solved — not tuned — against the constraint the implementation plan set ("Moon
and Mars unchanged within ~5%"), those being the two bodies the old 3.6 was fitted on and the only
two that were in range. Result at 1.9: **Moon −2.8%, Mars −0.5%**, and **nothing clips**.

## 4. Self-shadowing: honest about what it is not

The term darkens surfaces carrying real relief below ~7° of local solar elevation. It is **not**
shadow-mapping and does not ray-march the height field, so it cannot produce the individual cast
shadows a real terminator shows. What it reproduces is the _aggregate_ effect — that a relieved
surface near the terminator is darker than its reflectance law alone predicts, because a growing
fraction of it is occluded by its own topography.

Gated on `max(uHasHeight, uHasNormal)`, so the twelve bodies shipping neither are bit-identical.
**Verdict SIMPLIFIED**, and the declaration is the point: a viewer should not read this as resolved
terrain shadowing.

## 5. The atmosphere flag is correctly narrow

The Rayleigh + aerosol term is parameterised with **Earth's** sea-level optical depths (Bodhaine et
al. 1999) and Earth's maritime aerosol. Venus, Titan, Mars, Jupiter and Saturn all have real
atmospheres, and **none of them may set this flag** until the term carries their own τ — a 92-bar
CO₂ atmosphere is not a thin N₂/O₂ one scaled down. Shipping `{earth}` and nothing else is the
correct value set, and moving it from an inferred proxy to a declared flag is what makes that a
decision on the record rather than an accident of which maps happen to ship.

## 7. What this audit did NOT cover

- **Real-hardware appearance.** Every number here is computed or measured off the shipped assets;
  the screenshots in TR-087 are SwiftShader at the `balanced` tier. The `full`/`ultra-progressive`
  ladder rung was not exercised by any automated run on this machine and needs the owner's eyes.
- **Earth.** Its surface term brightens ~67% under the new pipeline, but Earth's appearance is
  dominated by the Rayleigh, cloud and glint terms, and it is unreachable until D6.4 lands. Audit
  it there, at the geometry it will actually be seen at.
- **The other eight bodies' absolute appearance.** Ranks and clipping are verified; "does Europa
  look right" is an owner judgement this audit cannot make.
