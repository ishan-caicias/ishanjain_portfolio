## What this slice unlocks, and why it needed a second sphere path

Every `travelTo` arrival in this scene is pinned to **phase angle 0.000°** — the camera parks on
the Sun-body line while `sunDirectionFrom` returns the negated body direction, so V = L
identically. That single fact is why no body in this scene has ever shown a terminator, a twilight
band, or a night side.

Earth escapes it, and only Earth can, because Earth is not a body here: the catalog frame is
**geocentric**, so Earth's direction is 0/0 and its distance is 0. Astra's §B3 is explicit that a
catalog entry with any ra/dec would be BROKEN PHYSICS, and the engine agrees three independent
ways (`bodyDepth(0)` would place it deeper than Neptune; `sunDirectionFrom(origin)` returns its
documented arbitrary fallback; `goHome` already owns the origin). So Earth is revealed by
`goHome` through a second, body-less sphere path, parked at **ra 160 / dec 0 = 90.0000° phase**.

## What shipped

**1. Night-light assets resurrected.** The pipeline's own `PACK_RECKONING` entry named the
precondition — _"Ship it if the arrival geometry ever changes"_ — and this is that change. The
entry is **preserved and superseded in place**, not deleted: its reasoning about `travelTo` was
never wrong, its precondition changed. New `MAP_TIER_CAP` gives per-map tier ceilings so `night`
ships base + high while every other Earth map keeps ultra, applied in one place shared by the
builder and `--verify` so the gate cannot disagree with what is emitted.

**2. Night-lights shader term, both twins.** Masked on `(1.0 - dayside)` — the exact complement of
the term that lights the day side, which is the specific thing Astra's broken-physics #1 requires;
floor-subtracted before the gain; attenuated by cloud alpha; and composited **in linear, before
the Reinhard curve**.

**3. Earth-at-origin reveal** with the sun direction read from the **Sun's own catalog entry**
(ra 250 / dec −20.5) rather than `sunDirectionFrom`, whose zero-length guard returns an arbitrary
`[0,0,1]` that would put the terminator wherever it happened to land.

**4. The R16 home orbit.** 180 s screen-time period, and the axis is the **Sun direction** — which
is the load-bearing choice, not a convenience: orbiting about that axis makes the 90° phase angle a
conserved quantity, so the terminator, twilight band and city lights hold for the whole revolution.
Any other axis sweeps the lighting through full and new phases.

**5. "Already home" redefined in both places it lived.** The old predicate was `|cam| < 1` and
appeared in `goHome`'s early-return _and_ `_tickWarp`'s idle-drift branch. The orbit sits at 38
units, so leaving either alone would have made every `goHome` press re-warp out of its own orbit.
Replaced with one shared `_isAtHomeVantage()`.

## Why the ordering mattered

D6.4 could not have preceded TR-087. City lights added **after** a tone map are additive in display
space and clip to flat white blobs with no structure (Astra §2.3). They have to be composited in
linear before the curve — so the curve had to exist first. A unit test now pins that ordering in
both twins rather than leaving it to be re-derived.

## Three real defects found during this slice

**1. The reveal fired at boot, putting the camera inside the planet.** My first predicate was
"not travelling and near the origin", which is true at boot — where `cam = [0,0,0]`, i.e. **inside
a sphere of radius 26** — and it also fetched ~2.5 MB of Earth textures during startup on every
page load, competing with the boot-critical set D1.1 measures. Caught by reading the probe output
rather than by a test: the E2E I had already written _asserted the buggy behaviour_, because I
wrote it from what I observed instead of from the contract. Fixed by requiring the orbit to be
armed — the delivery plan's own name for the slice is the "**goHome** reveal", and arriving home is
the trigger. The spec was corrected to assert the contract, and now fails against the old
behaviour.

**2. The orbit ran 8× slow.** Measured 1.5° in 6 s against the 12° a 180 s period implies. Root
cause: the idle branch used the `SHIP_MAX_DT`-clamped `dt` (0.05 s), so below 20 fps the phase
accumulates slower than wall clock — at SwiftShader's ~2.5 fps, exactly the 8× observed. **The fix
was already documented twenty lines below in the same function**, where the warp integration
explains that progress must track wall clock and only the physics step may be clamped. The orbit
phase is a progress quantity; switched to `_dtWarpS`, re-measured at 16° in ~6 s.

**3. A canvas `tabindex="1"` axe violation — CLAUDE.md #23.** Babylon's deferred pointer setup can
re-stamp the positive tabindex **before the first frame runs**, and the existing guard is a
per-frame re-assert, so an axe scan landing in that window sees a serious violation. The render
loop's own comment records this happening once before. It is a pre-existing race that this slice's
larger shader (longer effect compile → later first frame) makes more likely rather than one it
introduces — but #23 is a non-negotiable, so it is now closed properly with a `MutationObserver` on
the attribute, disconnected on teardown. An accessibility contract must not depend on frame pacing.

## Assets and the ADR-0009 ceiling

The night map compresses to almost nothing because it is mostly black: **0.25 MB total** (base
0.05 MB, high 0.20 MB), against the ~1.8 MB the owner approved and the ~6.4 MB three tiers would
have cost. Ceilings raised **169.5 → 170.0** (`assets/planets`) and **256.5 → 257.0** (total) with
the justification the gate itself demands, including a note that this line _retracts_ the earlier
ratchet-down that removed these very assets — the same rule working in the other direction.

## Named test change (CLAUDE.md #15)

`planet-asset-pipeline.test.ts`'s **"does NOT ship Earth's night lights, and records why"** is
inverted. It was the highest-value assertion in that file and said so, so the justification is
recorded at length in the test itself. In short: every word of the original reasoning is still true
about `travelTo`, and this change satisfies the precondition that reasoning itself named. The test
now pins the thing that actually protects the physics — not "no night map", but **"no night map
without the 90° vantage that earns it"** — plus the preserved reckoning entry, plus a new test that
**recomputes the 90° phase angle** from the Sun's catalog entry so the night lights lose their
licence the moment that geometry moves.

## Known limitations

- **The city lights have not been confirmed legible on real hardware.** Every automated check
  confirms the term is present, correctly masked on the true N·L, correctly ordered before the tone
  map, and that Earth renders with a real terminator (screenshot below). Whether the cities _read_
  depends on which longitude faces the camera — Earth rotates once per ~86 s of screen time — and
  on the `full`/ultra tier, which Playwright's SwiftShader environment never selects. **This is the
  headline visual of the slice and it needs the owner's eyes.**
- **The twilight band is unmeasured.** The brief predicts ~290 px at this geometry; nothing here
  checks it.
- **⚠ Earth's photometry is still fitted for α = 0.** `PLANET_LUNAR_L.earth = 0` and
  `PLANET_ALBEDO.earth = 0.213` were both derived at zero phase, and the Lunar-Lambert constant's
  own source comment warns it "becomes a 37-67% error the moment the arrival phase ever changes."
  D6.4 is that change. Not introduced here — inherited — but the realism review flags it as the one
  open item and recommends closing it with Astra's numbers **before D1.3 builds the ascent on this
  same geometry**.
- The orbit's rate was verified as order-of-magnitude correct against wall clock, not pinned to
  180 s exactly; the E2E asserts displacement and phase-hold rather than a rate, deliberately,
  because frame pacing in that environment is not something worth pinning.
