## P0-1 — DSO arrival overshoot

### The defect, quantified

`travelTo` parks at `body.pos − dir × standoff` where standoff was one of two constants: 80 for
planet/moon/dwarf, **38 for everything else including every nebula**. The raymarched volume has
radius `depth × 0.14` — **71–91 units** for the 11 shipped volumes. So the camera parked at 38
units from the core of a ~77-unit cloud: through the near wall, roughly half-way in, inside the
full-density shell (`shellInner: 0.55`). All 11 volumes landed in a 0.42–0.53 band.

Vega quantified the approach it produced: on the M42 run the gas rim subtends 57.7° at k=0.56 and
**109°** at k=0.70 — an invisible cloud (reveal 0.17) already overflowing the frame 3.3× — with
the camera inside the volume from k≈0.72. Her verdict: the largest remaining contradicting
closure cue in the scene.

### The fix, and why 3.5 is derived rather than chosen

New `NEBULA_ARRIVE_STANDOFF_FACTOR = 3.5`, applied as `volume.radius × 3.5` (250–320 units).

**This is the three-way seam resolving to a single value, not a compromise:**

| Source                        | Constraint                                                                                                                                | Result            |
| ----------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------- |
| **Astra** (physics)           | Defensible viewing band **2.5–4.0 R**; hard floor 1.5 R                                                                                   | 3.5 ✓             |
| **Vega** (engineering-forced) | `clampZoomDistance`'s floor is `restDist × ZOOM_MIN_MULT` (0.3), so **k ≥ 1/0.3 = 3.33** or the visitor zooms back inside the gas by hand | 3.5 ✓ (5% margin) |

3.5 is the only value satisfying both. At 3.5 R the rim subtends 33.2° = 0.71 of frame height,
and maximum hand zoom-in reaches 1.05 R — pressed against the near wall, still outside it.
**No new zoom-floor policy was needed**: the existing multiplicative floor moves with the
standoff for free. Both sisters independently identified this same constraint.

### Astra corrected the working hypothesis, and it changed the framing of the whole fix

Procyon's own triage assumed a close-range nebula would be effectively invisible (local gas
density ~10²–10⁴ cm⁻³ over a short path length). Astra confirmed the local-gas half — n_e = 10³
cm⁻³ over 1,000 km is **25.5 magnitudes** below M42's core — and then showed the conclusion does
not follow: **surface brightness is conserved along a ray**, and from inside M42 every sight-line
still crosses ~12.7 ly of the same cloud (40× the 0.1 pc needed), so the whole sky glows at
~0.75 mag below the Earth view. Inside you lose **structure**, not light.

**Consequence: the arrival premise is real physics, not a declared license.** Had the audit not
been run, this fix would have shipped with a fictional-feature caveat it does not need.

Astra did file the departure that IS real: the shipped volumes' angular sizes span **568:1** in
reality (Veil 3°, M42 65′, M57 84″×58.8″, MyCn 18 19″×8.5″) and all render at exactly 16.0959° —
`depth` cancels identically in `2·asin(0.14)`. Graded **DECLARED LICENSE**, previously
undeclared, now ledger entries **L19–L24**.

### Reveal envelope: tail only

Per Vega, `decelStart = 0.56` **must not move** — it is coupled to the D3.2 flip window
(ADR-0011) and `smooth01`'s zero onset slope already staggers the gas clear of the retro relight.
Only the tail changed: `decelMax` 0.7 → **0.85**, `arriveSwellS` 1.8 → **1.2**. Reason: the ship
now stops outside the cloud, so there is no final loom to carry a low arrival level — at 0.7 the
cloud was visibly still building while the arrival dossier was already up.

### Scope discipline: the branch keys on volume PRESENCE, never on type

Vega flagged this as an implementation trap and it is honoured in code and comment: the test is
`NEBULA_VOLUMES.find(v => v.id === b.e.id)`, not `e.t === "nebula"`. The catalogs carry far more
nebula-typed entries than the 11 rendered volumes; a type check would hand dozens of
billboard-only objects a standoff derived from a volume they do not have.

**Non-volumetric DSOs deliberately keep 38.** Their sprite is sized in _screen_ space and is
already at its 130-px clamp at 38 (the clamp engages at dist ≤ 40.2 for a legendary), so backing
off would be pure loss. Vega's general rule, now recorded: _world-space extent → a multiple of
it; screen-space extent → a fixed standoff._

## P0-2 — The abort control was painted over

`◂ RETURN HOME` **is** the mid-warp abort (ADR-0010). It was rendered, enabled and clickable
throughout every flight — while `WarpOverlay`'s bottom letterbox (`h-16` = 64px of opaque
`#05081a` at z-70) covered a bar docked at `bottom-8` (32px) at z-62. The bar occupied y=32…80px;
the letterbox owned y=656…720 (i.e. the bottom 64px). **The entire button row was covered.**

The _top_ letterbox was accounted for when it shipped (`HUD.tsx` parks its readouts at
`top-[clamp(72px,12vh,78px)]`); the bottom one never got the equivalent treatment.

### The fix is arithmetically forced, not a design choice

Clearing the letterbox needs `bottom ≥ 64`. Staying under the warp readout's old floor needs
`bottom + 48 ≤ 112`. **There is no solution** — so the readout's floor had to rise with the bar.
Both moved: console to `clamp(76px, 11vh, 92px)`, readout floor 112px → 148px (17vh → 20vh).

### A second-order bug the new test caught

The first CSS draft set only `bottom`. `body.ij-travel.ij-at-home` sets `top: clamp(45vh,54vh,…)`
with `bottom: auto`, and React clears `ij-at-home` a frame _after_ the warp starts — so for one
frame both were live, the element had `top` AND `bottom`, and stretched to **252px tall** instead
of its natural ~48px. Fixed with an explicit `top: auto` plus a doubled selector matching the
at-home rule's specificity, so the geometry never depends on which class React removes first.

### Reduced motion

`#ij-mission-bar`'s transition block is already disabled under `prefers-reduced-motion`, so the
lift becomes an instant reposition rather than a slide. The contract holds with **no new rule** —
verified by reading, not assumed.

## Test changes

**Additive — and both were negative-controlled, which is the part that matters:**

- `tests/unit/nebula-field.test.ts` (+4): every shipped volume is parked _outside_ its own gas
  **and stays outside at maximum hand zoom-in**; the old fixed 38 is asserted to have been
  _inside_ every one of them (so a future "simplification" back to a constant fails loudly); the
  factor is pinned inside Astra's band AND above Vega's zoom floor; the reveal is complete at
  rest with `decelStart` pinned unmoved.
- `tests/e2e/mid-warp-input.spec.ts` (+1): geometric occlusion guard — the console's and the
  button's bottom edges must clear the letterbox's top edge, and the readout must clear the
  lifted console.

**Two test hooks added to `WarpOverlay.tsx`** (`data-testid` on the bottom letterbox and the warp
readout), each with a comment explaining why a hit-test cannot substitute.

### The occlusion guard took three drafts, and the first two were wrong in instructive ways

Recorded because a green test that cannot fail is worse than no test:

1. **`elementFromPoint` — rejected before running.** The overlay is `pointer-events-none`, so
   `elementFromPoint` skips it entirely. It would have passed against the bug.
2. **Poll-then-query — timed out.** On SwiftShader a whole journey renders in ~10 frames, the
   overlay unmounts, and `boundingBox()` waits forever on an element legitimately gone. This
   spec's own file header warns about exactly this; the fix was to measure inside one
   `page.evaluate` off the page's own rAF, as every other spec in the file already does.
3. **Measured too early — VACUOUS, caught only by negative control.** Gated on the engine's
   first non-idle frame, but `cosmos:warp` → React is throttled to 10 Hz (D7.4), so
   `body.ij-warping` was not set yet; and `#ij-mission-bar` carries `transition: bottom 0.6s`, so
   the bar was still mid-flight. **With the fix reverted, this draft still passed.** Fixed by
   gating on the warp chrome being live _and_ waiting 750 ms for the transition to settle.

**Negative control, final version:** with `bottom` reverted to the pre-fix `2rem`, the guard
fails with `barBottom 688 > lbTop 656` — a 32px overlap, matching the hand arithmetic exactly.
The guard demonstrably catches the defect it was written for.

### PROCESS DEFECT, recorded because it invalidated a whole 46-minute suite run

**The negative control was performed by mutating the real source file, and the restore did not
survive.** The first full E2E run after the fix came back **174 passed / 3 failed**, and the
occlusion guard's failure reported `barBottom 688` — the _unfixed_ geometry. Cause: the negative
control edited `src/styles/global.css` in place, and although the restore ran and was verified by
`grep` at the time, the file was later observed still holding `bottom: 2rem`. The suite therefore
spent 46 minutes testing a build that did not contain the fix.

Two things went wrong and both are worth fixing as habits:

1. **A negative control must not mutate the file under test.** Do it on a scratch copy, or via
   `git stash`/`git diff` so the working tree is provably restored — not by `sed -i` plus a
   hand-rolled `cp` restore.
2. **Verify `dist/`, not just the source.** The check that actually caught this was
   `grep -o "ij-warping #ij-mission-bar[^}]*}" dist/_astro/*.css`. Source-level confidence is not
   evidence about what Playwright serves — Playwright serves the prebuilt `dist/` (CLAUDE.md's
   "a stale `dist/` silently tests old code", here reached by a different route).

A third, smaller instance of the same class in the same session: a rebuild silently no-opped
because `npm run build` is `lint && check && astro build` and an unformatted `TR-115.md` failed
lint first — CLAUDE.md #27's own corollary ("prettier the files you touched BEFORE
`npm run build`") applying to a _documentation_ file rather than source.

### Two TR-114 tests were racy and are fixed here (named changes, CLAUDE.md #15)

Both were written by this same agent in TR-114, passed then, and failed in this slice's full
suite on different timing. Neither is caused by the P0 changes; both are genuine test defects.

| Test                                                    | Defect                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                     | Fix                                                                                                   |
| ------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `render-console.spec.ts` "shrinking then re-growing…"   | Polled `gaiaTinyEnabledChunks === 2` then immediately read `gaiaTinyCount`. `_setGaiaTinyChunks` assigns the chunk index **before** the fetch/decode resolves, so `countAt2` captured **0** and the later `toBeLessThan(countAt2)` became unsatisfiable (`Expected < 0, Received 304947`). | Poll the decoded **count** > 0, then assert the chunk index separately. Strictly stronger.            |
| `render-console.spec.ts` "8-chunk ceiling from the URL" | A pure `clampLayerValue` assertion, but booting at the clamped value also fetches all 8 chunks (36.8 MB, ~2.44M records decoded on SwiftShader). Exceeded the 30 s default under full-suite load; passed in isolation.                                                                     | `test.setTimeout(90000)` — the budget now matches what the test actually does. No assertion weakened. |

No test was skipped, `fixme`'d, or had an assertion loosened.

## What this proves, and what it does not

**Proven:** the standoff is now derived from each volume's own rendered radius and is
unit-tested against every shipped volume including the zoom-floor edge; the abort control clears
the letterbox with a guard proven to fail without the fix; neither change moved a budget.

**NOT proven — needs owner eyes, and the sisters named the checks:**

- **Whether a 33.2° nebula reads as "arrived at" rather than "a wall of gas."** Vega's own
  acceptance list asks specifically: does the pull-back read as arrival, and does the
  photographic sprite at the cloud's centre read as a bright core or as a pasted-on sprite?
- **The two honest costs Vega recorded:** path length roughly halves, so warp streaks shorten
  (journey _duration_ is keyed to `ly` and is unchanged), and the closure cue drops from 13.7×
  looming to ~2×.
- **Reduced-motion flash.** Under reduced motion the gas still goes 0 → 1 in one frame across
  ~71% of frame height. Vega marked a ≤200 ms opacity-only fade as a **separable** refinement
  and explicitly said not to fold it into the P0 fix silently. **Not done here.** Recorded as a
  follow-up with her rationale intact.
