## Part 1 — The measurement that changed the phase

Before writing streaming code I checked whether virtual texturing would actually pay here. It is
the kind of question that is usually unanswerable, but this scene makes it arithmetic: the camera
parks at `ARRIVE_STANDOFF` = **38 world units** from a sphere of radius **26**, and there is **no
zoom, dolly or wheel handler anywhere in the engine**. The worst-case magnification is therefore a
constant.

```
sphere angular diameter   86.3°  against a 45.8° FOV   → the planet OVERFLOWS the screen
visible surface patch     23.5°  across
```

| Surface map           | Texels across the patch | Verdict                                 |
| --------------------- | ----------------------: | --------------------------------------- |
| 4096 — **as shipped** |                     268 | **4× magnified at 1080p, visibly soft** |
| 8192 — source         |                     536 | 2× magnified                            |
| VT level 3            |                   1,070 | ~1% under 1080p parity                  |
| VT level 4            |                   2,143 | ~1% under 4K parity                     |
| VT level 5            |                   4,286 | **beyond what this camera can resolve** |

Three consequences, none of which I would have reached by reasoning:

1. **C4.1 shipped a visibly soft surface and I did not know it.** The owner confirmed Mars, Mercury
   and the Moon "look good", and they do — but the map was being stretched 4×. That is a real
   defect found by measuring something already accepted.
2. **Virtual texturing genuinely pays.** This is not gold-plating; the shipped map really is short.
3. **Level 5 is never built.** It is 2,048 tiles and the bulk of the source pack's 105 MB, and not
   one of its texels is reachable. Levels 0–4 are the entire useful pyramid — a ~75% saving decided
   by arithmetic rather than by taste.

**The unit tests caught me overstating this table twice.** The first draft claimed level 3
"satisfies 1080p" and level 4 "satisfies 4K". Both are ~1% _under_ their nominal targets — the
pyramid doubles and 23.5° does not divide neatly into it. `levelForViewport` now steps **up** on a
marginal miss rather than treating close-enough as satisfied, and the table says what is true.

## Part 2 — What shipped

**Ultra surface tier (8192×2048→4096).** Directly addresses the 4× magnification above: the source
maps were already 8192 wide, so this costs disk and nothing else, and it is the single largest
visible improvement available to this phase.

**Progressive texture load.** `high` (≈1 MB) lands fast and gets the body on screen; `ultra`
(4–6 MB) swaps in when it arrives. Guarded on the destination not having changed mid-fetch, or
arriving elsewhere would paint the wrong planet onto the sphere. This also softens the grey-ball
loading state TR-076 flagged.

**`NEVER_SPHERE` — Phobos and Deimos, enforced.** The owner's decision recorded as a rule. It is
enforced rather than honoured by omission specifically because the pipeline is data-driven: a body
becomes spherical the moment the manifest contains it, so without an explicit block, adding
`phobos-high.jpg` later would silently sphere a potato.

**Real rotation on its own clock.** Real sidereal periods (Venus and Pluto retrograde), with
`ROTATION_TIME_ACCEL = 1e3` — deliberately **not** the belt's 4e5. Astra: at 4e5 Mars would spin
4.5 revolutions per second, 27° per frame, past Nyquist, and would visibly alias _backwards_. At
1e3 Mars takes ~89 s per revolution. Because it is one scalar, every real period ratio is exact.
The scene now runs two clocks 400× apart; that is declared, and they are never in frame together.

**The VT pipeline, complete and tested.** `planet-vt.ts` (pure tile selection — grid, level choice,
sub-camera UV, patch coverage, seam and pole dedup) and `build-planet-vt.mjs`, which baked **1,364
normal tiles across levels 0–4 for Mars and the Moon**.

**Normals are baked offline, not streamed as heights** — Astra's finding, and the reason the bake
script exists at all. The packs are 8-bit JPEG; at level 5 the vertical quantization implies a
**14.4° minimum representable slope**, and a real level-5 tile was measured spanning just **14
distinct byte values across its whole 1024²**. Differentiating that live terraces the surface.
Quantization is catastrophic in heights (differentiated) and benign in normals (consumed
directly), so the differentiation happens once, offline, with a ±4-texel Sobel.

The bake also **corrects the source data**: Gaia Sky's own `heightScale` renders the Moon 3.6× and
Mercury 7.7× too flat against real relief. Because the script bakes slopes rather than passing
heights through, it uses the real figures instead.

## Part 3 — Astra's Venus brief, and the finding that reshapes that feature

Delivered this session, ahead of implementation. Two measured asset findings and one design call
that matters more than the feature it is about:

- **`venus-cloud.jpg` is a UV map, not a visible-light one.** All three channels share identical
  statistics — one band, not three — and its contrast is **20.5%** against real Venus visible-light
  cloud contrast of **1–3%**. Compositing it at full contrast would present a UV image as a
  photograph.
- **`venus-ultra.jpg`'s orange tint (1.00, 0.57, 0.20) is the real Venera illuminant.** Astra
  derived that colour independently from Rayleigh transmission and it matched to two decimals. The
  tint is defensible; the spatial contrast is not.
- **The real dishonesty risk is not the radar map — it is LIGHTING the radar map.** C4.1's
  directional Sun, Lunar-Lambert reflectance and height-derived normals applied to Magellan data
  would manufacture fake geometry and then fake lighting on it, and would pass every review. Astra
  mandates a body-specific shader fork below ~45 km: no directional light, no terminator,
  `venus-normal.jpg` never bound, self-shadowing off. That is also the physically correct answer —
  Venera recorded flat, shadowless orange light with no solar disc.

This **supersedes** C4.1's brief §6 recommendation to "render the bland cloud deck", recorded as a
reversal rather than edited away.

Key constants for when it is built: cloud top **70 km** (entry), **cloud base 47.5 km — the
breakout, and the real payoff, not 0 km**; **do not land** (at the surface you can see 3.3 km and
nothing else); **20 s** total, 50% of it inside the deck; cloud-top wind **100 m/s** which must
advect at **real time 1.0×**, since on the 1e3 rotation clock it would reach 0.033 c.

## Part 4 — An asset-size finding that needs an owner decision

`public/assets` is now **221 MB**:

| Asset group             |  Size |
| ----------------------- | ----: |
| VT normal tiles (1,364) | 63 MB |
| Planet surface/height   | 57 MB |
| SDSS DR18 galaxy field  | 47 MB |
| Everything else         | 54 MB |

Runtime cost is not the issue — every one of these is fetched on demand, and the VT streamer would
pull ~16 tiles (≈0.7 MB) for a given view. The cost is **repo and deploy weight**.

**And there is a real question about whether level 4 earns its 50 MB.** The ultra _colour_ map
gives 536 texels across the patch. Level 3 normals give 1,070 — already **2× the colour
resolution**. Level 4 gives 2,143 — **4×**. Shading detail at four times the resolution of the
colour it shades has strongly diminishing returns, because the eye reads the combination. Dropping
to level 3 would cut the VT tiles from 63 MB to about 12 MB.

I have not made that call, because it trades the owner's stated goal 1 (maximum realism) against
goal 2 (performance/weight) and the numbers are close enough to be a judgement rather than a
calculation. **Recorded for decision, not silently resolved.**

## Part 6 — WHERE I STOPPED, AND WHY

**The runtime tile streamer is not wired.** The pipeline, the tile math and the baked assets are
all done and tested; nothing consumes them yet.

This is a deliberate stop, not an oversight. Correct multi-tile virtual texturing needs a tile
atlas plus an indirection lookup, because the visible patch straddles up to **16 tiles** and the
naive alternatives are worse than nothing: binding only the nearest tile leaves 15/16 of the view
without detail behind a hard visible boundary, and binding four fixed samplers seams in exactly
the cases the visitor is most likely to look at. Half-built virtual texturing looks like a
rendering defect, and this repo's own doctrine is to prefer the smallest coherent surface over a
partial one.

**Consequence to be explicit about: the 63 MB of baked tiles currently ship unused.** They are the
input to the next increment and the bake is deterministic and re-runnable in minutes, so they can
equally be deleted and regenerated later. That choice is coupled to the Part 4 decision.

## Part 7 — What remains before PF-10 is complete

- **C4.2 runtime streamer** — atlas + indirection. Design settled, assets baked.
- **C4.2 Venus cloud descent** — Astra's brief is in hand, including the shader-fork mandate.
- **C4.3** — self-shadowing near the terminator (Astra: a 3 km rim at 1° solar elevation casts
  172 km of shadow, ~50 hard black pixels on a 1000-px Moon — the real tell that displacement is
  missing is the absent _shadows_, not the absent silhouette), and tier gating.
- **Asset-weight decision** (Part 4).
- **Unchanged from earlier phases:** C2's and C3's real-device frame-budget gates, and the asteroid
  belt's 23.44° frame decision.

**Owner-eyes outstanding for this session's work:** whether the ultra tier visibly sharpens Mars,
Mercury and the Moon against what was confirmed earlier, and whether rotation at ~89 s per Mars
revolution reads as a turning world or as a fidget. The embedded browser pane has failed to drive
this scene three sessions running, so neither was checked by eye here.
