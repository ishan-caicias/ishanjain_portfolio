# TR-076 — PF-10 C4.1: real planetary spheres, and the plan premise that did not survive contact

**Date:** 2026-07-21
**Scope:** PF-10 phase C4, "Texture & surface-data upgrade". The phase as written could not be
implemented, for a reason the plan had wrong at its foundation; this report records the finding,
the owner's re-scope, and the first sub-phase delivered against it.

**Verdict: OVERALL: READY TO PROCEED** — see Part 6. **C4.1 is complete; C4.2 (virtual-texture
streaming) and C4.3 (rotation, self-shadowing, tier gating) are not started.**

---

## Part 1 — The finding: there was nothing to enhance

PF-10's C4 section says elevation should be applied "to the existing Mars mesh," and calls the
phase "the lowest-risk — enhances existing meshes, adds no new interactive objects."

**There is no Mars mesh. There has never been a planet mesh of any kind.** Every curated body in
this scene — all 267 with photographic imagery — renders as a **billboard quad** sampling a
**128×128 cell** of the shared 4096×4096 `atlas.jpg` (`celestial-bodies.ts`, GAP-02). Measured,
not inferred: Mars's atlas cell is `[0.78125, 0.96875, 0.03125, 0.03125]`, and 0.03125 × 4096 =
128 px.

A flat camera-facing sprite cannot carry topography, cannot have a terminator, and cannot show a
phase. So C4 is not the smallest phase in PF-10 — it is the one that **introduces planetary
surface rendering to this engine**, which makes it the largest.

The topography packs compound it. Both are **virtual textures**, not images: Mars ships 6 levels
of 1024×1024 tiles (2,048 tiles at level 5, a 64K equirectangular pyramid); the Moon ships 5. A
tile pyramid presupposes a sphere, UV mapping, and a streamer with camera-distance LOD selection.
None of that existed either.

This is the same class of finding as TR-065's NGC2000 blocker — a plan step whose premise
dissolves on contact with the real renderer — and it was surfaced to the owner before any code was
written rather than quietly redefined.

**Owner decision (2026-07-21):** re-scope to **spheres + full virtual texturing**, chosen over
high-res close-up billboards and over spheres-with-single-height-maps. Since spheres are the
mandatory first step of that path regardless, the work is sequenced C4.1 → C4.2 → C4.3, and this
report covers C4.1.

## Part 2 — What C4.1 ships

**One destination-gated sphere, not one per body.** The visitor can only be in one place. Rather
than twenty-odd meshes each holding two 4096×2048 textures, almost all off-screen forever, a
single sphere is repositioned and re-textured for whichever body is the current destination —
following the arrival-keyed reveal mechanism ADR-0004 established for nebulae and TR-072
generalized. It also holds one body's textures in GPU memory instead of twenty, leaves the
267-billboard path completely untouched (no regression surface), and gives C4.2's streamer only
ever one body to stream.

**Equirectangular UV came free.** Babylon's `CreateSphereVertexData` emits exactly the mapping the
source maps use — the same convention `milky-way.ts` already depends on and documents. No
reprojection anywhere in this feature.

**Asset pipeline** (`scripts/build-planet-textures.mjs`): the real 8192×4096 equirect maps
downsampled to shipped `base` (2048×1024) and `high` (4096×2048) tiers, height maps written
greyscale since they carry one channel of real information. **12 bodies, 16.6 MB, 3 with real
elevation** — Mars, Moon and Mercury are the only bodies in the pack shipping a height map besides
Earth, which has elevation but no surface map.

**Two CLAUDE.md non-negotiables bound this directly, and both are load-bearing here rather than
ceremonial:**

- **#9 (TR-059)** — most of these bodies have no height map at all, so the height sampler's 1×1
  placeholder is the common case, not an edge case. On WebGPU an unbound binding throws while
  building the bind group and kills `scene.render()` for the entire frame.
- **#8 (TR-047)** — the four height samples are taken unconditionally and gated by multiplication
  with `uHasHeight`, never wrapped in an `if`. Branching around `textureSample` compiles happily
  on WebGL2 and is rejected on WebGPU — a twin divergence with no compiler to catch it. A unit
  test asserts the absence of that `if`.

## Part 3 — Astra's brief changed the physics, including one broken-physics call

Delegated before implementation. Three corrections were applied.

**BROKEN PHYSICS — Lambertian shading on airless bodies.** Not a simplification, an error. Real
regolith backscatters: the full Moon is a flat, evenly-lit disc with no limb darkening, and the
half Moon is only **~9% as bright as the full Moon** rather than the 50% Lambert predicts. Now
Lunar-Lambert, `I = albedo(2L·μ₀/(μ₀+μ) + (1−L)μ₀)`, with L = 1.0 for the airless Moon and
Mercury and 0.55 for Mars. This is why the shader takes a camera position at all — the
Lommel-Seeliger term needs the real emission angle, not just the incidence angle.

**Elevation exaggeration: 12 → 1.0.** The draft reasoned that since relief is a tiny fraction of a
planet's radius (Olympus Mons is 0.65% of Mars's), exaggeration must be mandatory. That conflates
two things. Relief is invisible on the **silhouette** — Astra measured that Mars must render
**2,260 px across** before topography bends the limb by one pixel — but shading depends on
**slope**, which is scale-free and already vivid at true scale. Exaggeration buys a sub-pixel limb
nobody sees and corrupts the shading across the whole disc, which is the part you look at. Astra
puts ≥3.0 in lie territory. The tell: real planetary limbs are clean arcs in every spacecraft
image ever taken.

**Albedos: Bond → geometric.** The draft's 0.25 (Mars) and 0.12 (Moon) are Bond albedos — total
scattered fraction, not what a renderer wants. Geometric albedos are **0.170** and **0.136**.

**Longitude origin.** The pack puts 180°E at u = 0, not 0°E. Astra pinned this by locating Mars's
real Hellas basin and Tharsis rise in the actual height map rather than trusting the convention.
Without the half-turn every body renders with the wrong face toward the Sun.

**Recorded, not yet acted on** (C4.2/C4.3): Gaia Sky's own `heightScale` for the Moon and Mercury
renders them **3.6× and 7.7× too flat** against real relief; the MOLA pyramid is 8-bit JPEG, whose
vertical quantization at level 5 implies a **14.4° minimum slope step** (a measured level-5 tile
spans just 14 byte values across 1024²), so normals should be baked offline rather than derived
live at that resolution; and sharing the belt's `TIME_ACCEL = 4e5` for rotation would spin Mars at
4.5 rev/s — past Nyquist, aliasing backwards. Rotation needs its own ~1e3 clock.

**Astra also corrected two of my own premises**: `tex/base/` holds 56 files, not the 105 I
reported (I counted the whole tree), and Callisto/Enceladus/Uranus/Neptune textures are referenced
by the pack's own JSON but absent from disk. I had independently caught Callisto while building
the pipeline.

## Part 4 — A source-data trap caught by reading the files instead of the listing

The pack ships **both** `satrun-high.jpg` and `saturn-high.jpg`. The obvious read — "upstream
typo, use the one that exists" — is wrong: they are different files, 1800×900 and 4096×2048
respectively, both genuine 2:1 equirectangular Saturn maps. This script's first draft treated the
typo as the only spelling and would have shipped Saturn at less than half resolution. Checking the
actual image dimensions rather than the filename listing caught it.

## Part 5 — What the automated suite proves, and what it does not

The E2E assertion is behavioural (non-negotiable #18), not a readiness check: travel to Mars
through the real state machine, then prove a sphere is **actually visible**, dressed as the right
body, material ready, console-clean. It also asserts the sphere is **hidden** before any
destination — a sphere floating at the origin would otherwise pass a naive "does it exist" test.
And it asserts `planetBodiesAvailable > 0` first, because a failed manifest fetch would make every
later assertion vacuous.

**A process note worth recording, because it happened twice this session.** Two E2E runs silently
executed against a **stale `dist`**: `npm run build` is `lint && check && astro build`, so a
Prettier failure short-circuits the build while Playwright happily serves the previous one and
reports passes. Both times it was caught by reading the command's full output rather than its last
line. The first C4 run "passed" against a build predating Astra's corrections entirely. Re-run
against a verified-fresh build afterwards.

**UPDATE (2026-07-21, same day) — owner-eyes confirmation received.** The owner tested the live
page and confirmed **Mars, Mercury and the Moon all read correctly**. That closes the visual
question this section left open: the terminator, the true-scale topography shading, and the
`PLANET_SPHERE_RADIUS = 26` sizing are all owner-accepted as shipped. Astra's exaggeration
correction (12 → 1.0) therefore stands vindicated in practice as well as in physics — the concern
that true-scale relief would look flat did not materialise.

**Not verified by eye at the time of writing.** The embedded browser pane again failed to drive this scene — `arrivedId`
never advanced and the renderer stopped responding to screenshot requests, the same instrument
limitation TR-075 recorded. The real-Chromium E2E is the proof that it renders; **how it looks is
owner-eyes outstanding**, and specifically worth checking: whether the terminator reads as a real
day/night boundary, whether Mars's topography is visible in the shading at true scale, and whether
`PLANET_SPHERE_RADIUS = 26` makes the body feel like a world or like a prop.

## Part 7 — What is still open

- **C4.2** — virtual-texture streaming (the 2,048-tile MOLA pyramid, the Moon's 512). Astra's
  8-bit quantization finding says normals should be baked offline at that resolution rather than
  derived live; that shapes the design.
- **C4.3** — rotation on its own ~1e3 clock, height-map self-shadowing near the terminator
  (Astra: a 3 km rim at 1° solar elevation casts 172 km of shadow — 50 hard black pixels on a
  1000-px Moon, and the real tell that displacement is missing is the absent shadows, not the
  absent silhouette), and tier gating from real-device data.
- **Body selection — now decided by the owner (2026-07-21), pending implementation in C4.2:**
  - **Phobos and Deimos: never sphered.** Astra's advice accepted. They are the most famously
    irregular bodies in the pack; a sphere there would be broken physics, so they stay billboards
    permanently. This is a rule, not a deferral. Neither is currently in the pipeline's body list,
    so nothing ships wrong today — but the exclusion should become explicit and tested rather than
    incidental.
  - **Venus: sphere it, and fly through the clouds.** Rather than dropping Venus or showing a
    radar map that pretends to be a view, the owner's call is to make the contradiction the
    feature — a cloud-descent on arrival that passes through the real Venusian cloud deck and
    emerges over the radar-mapped surface beneath. That is honest about what the data is (a
    Magellan radar map is exactly how humans _have_ seen Venus's surface — through the clouds, by
    instrument) and it turns the phase's most awkward asset into its most memorable arrival. Needs
    an Astra brief of its own (real cloud-deck altitude and depth, real super-rotation, what the
    descent would actually look like) before design.
- **PF-10's older open items are unchanged**: C2's and C3's real-device gates, and the asteroid
  belt's 23.44° frame decision.
