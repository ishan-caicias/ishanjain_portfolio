## What this slice is

Pressing **LAUNCH** plays an ~8 s Earth-surface → space ascent: the camera climbs radially from
just above Earth's surface to the home vantage while the sky bleeds from daylight blue to black,
the stars come out, and a thin blue limb ring crosses the Kármán line — then it hands off,
seamlessly, into the D6.4 home orbit and reveals the Where-To console into its dock. **It is the
D6.4 home reveal played forward**: same sphere, same 90° terminator geometry, same final camera
pose. Post-launch and post-`goHome` are the same frame — which is the point, and the backbone of
the whole design.

## What shipped

**New pure module `src/lib/ascent.ts`** (Astra §4 physics + the motion-spec curves, unit-testable
off-GPU): two deliberately different eases — `easeCam = k³` (thrust: barely moves early, leaves
fast) and `easeAlt = k²(3−2k)` (the sky darkens _ahead_ of the camera, which is both real and the
right dramatic order); `skyMix(h) = exp(−h/8.5)` keyed to **physical altitude**, not the camera;
`starFade` / `limbIntensity` thresholds; and the limb-glow shader twins.

**Engine (`babylon-engine.ts`):** a new `warp.mode = "ascent"` with `_tickAscent` driving one frame
— camera position along the home-vantage sight-line at the eased standoff, sky colour mutated onto
`scene.clearColor` (no per-frame allocation), band `uFade` driven by `starFade`, and a limb-glow
shell (a fresnel-rim sphere just outside Earth, additive, sun-gated so only the sunlit limb glows).
`beginAscent()` / `skipAscent()` public methods; the progress integrates **wall-clock `_dtWarpS`**,
not the clamped `dt` (the D6.4 orbit lesson — a screen-time quantity clamped to `SHIP_MAX_DT`
stretches on a slow device rather than speeding up). Handoff re-arms the home orbit and emits
`cosmos:ascent-done`. Reduced motion / no-WebGL is an instant cut. `travelTo`/`goHome`/`randomBody`
now also guard against `mode === "ascent"` so a stray click can't interrupt the cinematic.

**Host (`SpaceScene.tsx` + `PreFlight.tsx` + new `AscentSkip.tsx`):** LAUNCH triggers `beginAscent`
and the console reveal now **waits for `cosmos:ascent-done`** (separated `launched` from a new
`revealReady`), so the cinematic plays unobstructed. Only the real LAUNCH press plays the ascent —
SKIP INTRO, PROCEED ANYWAY and the returning-visitor bypass pass `ascent: false` and go straight to
content (a skip's whole value is speed, not a second animation). A full-screen `AscentSkip` overlay
(SKIP ▸ + Esc / Space / click) is skippable at every moment (D1-AC3); a skip is a jump-to-end.

**CSS (`global.css`):** the at-home dock formalised to `top: clamp(45vh, 54vh, calc(66vh − 56px))`
(the experience review's spec) so the ascent reveal and a later `goHome` land the console
identically; a fade + 12 px rise reveal (0.6 s, the vista's arrival easing); mobile bottom-dock with
`env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`.

## The one escalation the audit raised

The D6.4 review flagged that Earth's `PLANET_LUNAR_L = 0` and albedo `0.213` were both fitted at
α = 0, that Earth now renders at α = 90°, and that it should be re-derived "before D1.3 builds the
ascent." **D1.3 built it without that re-derivation** — so the audit escalates rather than
re-defers: the ascent is correct in everything it _added_, but it is now the **second** feature
sitting on one inherited constant the Earth brief itself predicts is a 37–67 % error at this angle.
It blocks nothing already shipped (both features are console-clean), but it should block the next
Earth-visual change, and a fair judgement of the ascent's own polish (terminator softness, the
~290 px twilight band) waits on it. **Wants Astra's numbers** — carried into D6's remaining debt.

### E2E — new `tests/e2e/ascent.spec.ts` (5 specs)

LAUNCH plays the ascent and hands off to the home vantage at 90° phase, console-clean, with the
console hidden _during_ the climb and revealed after; **D1-AC3** the SKIP affordance appears during
the ascent and activating it reveals content; **D1-AC6** reduced motion is an instant cut with the
console already visible; **D1-AC2** keyboard-only Tab→LAUNCH→Enter reaches a revealed, focusable
console; the returning-visitor bypass skips the cinematic on reload.

**Full-suite gate — two full runs.** Run 1 surfaced 3 failures, all in `preflight.spec.ts` (D1.2)
and all one root cause — a **named test change** (CLAUDE.md #15): D1.2's tests asserted
`ij-loading` clears _immediately_ on LAUNCH, but D1.3 now inserts the ~8 s ascent between LAUNCH
and the reveal. The animated reveal is covered by `ascent.spec.ts`; the D1.2 tests still assert the
same end contract (PreFlight dismisses at once, content reveals) with the ascent's time budget
added, and the returning-visitor test no longer waits out the first launch (the sessionStorage flag
is set synchronously in PreFlight's own `launch()`). No assertion was weakened — only the reveal's
timing window widened to match the cinematic that now precedes it. **Run 2 (final, after the named
changes): 117 / 118** — the one remaining failure is D3.2's GAP-17 flip-window flake, which
**passed in isolation** immediately after and has appeared-and-passed intermittently across every
recent TR (081/082/084/086/087). All D1.3 work — the 5 new ascent specs, the 5 fixed preflight
specs, Mars/Venus/accessibility — is green.

**Measured ascent duration:** ~8 s on real hardware (wall-clock driven), **~12–14 s on
SwiftShader** (two timing probes: prog 0.799 at 12 s; prog 0.649 at 9.7 s → done at 14.1 s) — the
dt cap stretches it on slow hardware and never speeds it up, so it can never become a strobe-fast
cut. `stats()` evals take multiple seconds each under the rendering load, which is why the E2E
detects ascent state through the synchronously-rendered SKIP overlay rather than a stats poll.

## A real test-design lesson (recorded, not just fixed)

The skip test first failed with "element detached from the DOM" — the full-screen SKIP overlay
unmounted mid-click. Root cause was **not** the engine: `sceneStats()` evals take _multiple seconds_
each under SwiftShader's rendering load (one measured at 12 s), so a `stats()`-based precheck for
"ascent is active" consumed enough wall-clock that the ascent completed inside the precheck. Fixed
by removing the slow precheck entirely — the SKIP overlay appears **synchronously** on LAUNCH (React
state, no eval), so waiting for _it_ and clicking lands early in the ascent. The test now asserts the
outcome D1-AC3 actually requires (input during the cinematic → visible response → revealed scene)
rather than trying to catch the ascent in a narrow, eval-latency-dependent window.

## Known limitations

- **The ascent needs the owner's eyes on real hardware.** Every physics claim is unit-asserted and
  the SwiftShader E2E confirms it runs console-clean and lands at 90° phase — but the _look_ of the
  blue→black transition, the felt timing of the star reveal, and the limb arc all need a real GPU at
  the `full` tier, which Playwright never selects.
- **The delivery plan's pre-build UX gate was not run.** The two cheapest research-plan validations
  (LAUNCH-gate hallway test ×5, dossier-vs-spinner probe) are human studies — flagged for the owner,
  not blocking the code.
- **⚠ Earth's α = 90° photometry** (the escalation above) — inherited, not introduced, but two
  features now depend on it.
- The limb ring is a fresnel-rim simplification, not a scattering integral (declared, ledger L10).
- On a slow device the ascent runs longer than 8 s (the wall-clock dt cap) — intentional; never
  faster, so it can never become a strobe-fast cut.
