## Part 1 — Writing the reckoning down is what proved it was wrong

The owner's first deviation was procedural: C4 shipped 12 of the pack's bodies and skipped the rest
by judgement that was never recorded, so "76 objects → 12 bodies" read as an omission.

The intended fix was to write the justification down. Doing that found that **two of its premises
were false**, which is the part worth recording — the missing artefact was not merely untidy, it
was load-bearing, and its absence had been hiding real missing work for three sessions.

| Claim on record (TR-076/077/078)                                             | Measured this session                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| "Earth has height-but-no-surface"                                            | **False.** `earth-day-ultra` is a real 6×**8192²** cubemap, plus `earth-night-ultra`, `earth-cloud-high`, `earth-specular-high`. The pipeline only ever globbed `tex/base/`; Earth's surface is the one body under `tex/cubemap/`. |
| "3 with real elevation — the only bodies in the pack that ship a height map" | **Incomplete.** Ten bodies ship pre-baked **normal** maps. Reading "ships a height map" as the test for "has real elevation" left nine bodies rendering as smooth spheres with their relief data sitting unused on disk.           |
| 12 bodies is the pack                                                        | **False.** dione (4096), rhea (**8192**) and tethys (4096) ship real surface _and_ normal maps. Nothing ruled them out — they were not in the list.                                                                                |

The generalisable finding: **the search was wrong, not the data.** Every skip was justified against
the file listing that had been looked at. Nobody had established that the listing was the pack.

### The reckoning is now executable, not prose

`PACK_RECKONING` in `scripts/build-planet-textures.mjs` maps every non-shipped source file to the
reason it is not shipped, and `--verify` **fails** if any file in the real pack is neither consumed
nor carries a recorded reason. A prose reckoning would have decayed at the next pack update; this
one fails the build instead. It currently accounts for all **64 pack entries — 31 shipped, 33
recorded as skipped** (the night-lights maps moved from shipped to recorded once Astra's brief
landed; see Part 7).

Two entries matter more than the rest, because they are deferrals rather than exclusions and would
otherwise simply disappear: the real **Planck CMB all-sky plates** and the photographic **Milky Way
skybox**. Both are genuine, usable data; both are out of C4's scope because they are background
layers rather than planetary surfaces; both now have somewhere to be found.

## Part 2 — Normal maps are not a lesser height map, they are a better one

Nine bodies (io, europa, ganymede, titan, pluto, ceres, dione, rhea, tethys) now ship real relief
through a new `normalTex` path in **both shader twins**.

The reason this is an upgrade rather than a compromise is the same finding `build-planet-vt.mjs`
already rests on, applied one level up: **8-bit quantization is catastrophic under differentiation
and benign under direct consumption.** The existing height path differentiates a greyscale map into
slopes; a baked normal map is consumed directly. Where the pack ships both forms it is strictly
better information, and C4's first pass discarded it.

`height` and `normal` are **mutually exclusive across the entire pack** — a real property of the
source data, not a convention imposed here — which is why the shader gates them against each other
with one `uHasNormal` and needs no precedence rule. Asserted by test rather than assumed.

**Two non-negotiables bound this and both are honoured explicitly:**

- **TR-047** — the normal sample is taken unconditionally and gated by `mix()`, never by an `if`.
  A branch around `textureSample` compiles on WebGL2 and is rejected on WebGPU.
- **TR-059** — `normalTex` always has a real texture bound, and it needed its **own** placeholder
  rather than the material's shared mid-grey. This is the one case in this material where the
  shared placeholder would be actively wrong rather than merely gated: a normal map decodes as
  `rgb * 2 - 1`, so (128,128,128) decodes to the **zero vector**, not to "no change". The neutral
  normal is (128,128,255). The shader survives either way — `max(nrm.z, 0.05)` keeps the
  normalize finite — which is precisely why it was worth fixing rather than leaving as code that
  is accidentally not broken.

### The backtick trap recurred, from the other direction

TR-078 Part 5 recorded a shell-quoting mistake corrupting a source file mid-patch via backticks in
template literals. It happened again here, from a different cause: a **comment** inside the GLSL
template literal quoted two identifiers as `` `height` `` and `` `normal` ``, terminating the
literal and turning the rest of the shader into TypeScript. Caught immediately by `astro check`.

There is now a unit test asserting **no shader source string contains a backtick at all**, which
converts a recurring hazard into a gate. Prose inside these strings uses plain words.

## Part 3 — Earth, and an orientation proof rather than an eyeball

Earth's surface required re-projecting a cubemap into this pipeline's equirectangular convention
(`scripts/lib/cubemap-equirect.mjs`). Two things about that are worth recording.

**Orientation was measured, not assumed.** A cubemap has 24 plausible per-face orientations and the
failure mode is quiet — continents in roughly the right places with one face rotated still reads as
"Earth" at a glance. The same pack ships `earth-specular-high.jpg`, an 8192×4096 **equirect** ocean
mask in the convention every other map here uses, which makes it a ground-truth land mask with
known orientation. `scripts/validate-cubemap-orientation.mjs` correlates the re-projected day map
against it and scores the plausible wrong variants alongside:

| Variant                      |           r |
| ---------------------------- | ----------: |
| **shipped**                  | **−0.5995** |
| mirrored (longitude flipped) |     −0.2781 |
| half-turn in longitude       |     −0.2658 |
| flipped (latitude inverted)  |     −0.2003 |

Land is strongly anti-correlated with an ocean specular mask, as it must be, and the shipped
convention wins by more than 2×. The script exits non-zero if it ever stops winning.

**A filtering defect, caught and corrected in the same session.** The first draft sampled the cube
faces bilinearly. Each face spans exactly 90° of azimuth, so it lands on `width / 4` pixels of the
equirect — an 8192-px face feeding an 8192-wide equirect is a **4× downscale**, and bilinear taps
2×2 of that 4×4 footprint, which does not filter it but **aliases** it, worst along coastlines.
Faces are now pre-reduced by a real Lanczos kernel to `width / 2` before sampling. `width / 2`
rather than the naive `width / 4` because the gnomonic mapping is not uniform: angular density at a
face centre is exactly 2× that at its edge, so the naive size would correctly filter the centre
while softening the edges — where four faces meet and softness reads as a seam.

The correction moved the 8192 Earth from 1.88 MB to 1.77 MB, a 6% **drop**, which is the direction
that confirms the diagnosis: the extra bytes were encoding aliasing noise. Recorded as the sign
check it is, not as proof of correctness — the orientation correlation and the geometry unit tests
are the proof.

## Part 4 — The regeneration hazard, and the constraint that reframed it

The owner's second deviation: `build-planet-textures.mjs` and `build-planet-vt.mjs` followed the
`assets:craft` conventions but were never registered in `package.json`, so C4's assets were
effectively unreproducible without reading a TR to find the command.

Investigating the fix surfaced a constraint that changes what "auto-run in CI" can even mean:
**`resources/` is gitignored, so CI can never regenerate any asset in this repo** — not the craft
GLBs, not the celestial PNG-packs, not the planet textures. The committed bytes under
`public/assets` _are_ the deliverable. Rebuilding in CI is impossible by construction.

What CI _can_ assert is that the committed bytes still match what the pipelines declare they
produce. Both scripts therefore gained the three-mode shape `build-craft-assets.mjs --verify`
established:

| Mode         | Behaviour                                                                                                                |
| ------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| (no flag)    | full rebuild; requires the source pack                                                                                   |
| `--if-stale` | rebuilds only what is missing or older than its source; **no-ops with a clear message** when the pack is absent          |
| `--verify`   | **source-free**: declared body/tier/map present and non-empty, manifests consistent, no orphans, no NEVER_SPHERE sphered |

Wired as `assets:planets`, `assets:planets:vt`, `assets:sync`, `assets:verify`,
`assets:cubemap:validate`. `predev`/`prepreview` run `assets:sync`; `prebuild` runs
`assets:verify`; CI's **lint** job runs `assets:verify` and `budget:check:assets` as steps in the
existing job rather than as new jobs, keeping the branch-protection ruleset's required
status-check names valid — the same reason `docs:check` lives there (TR-034).

Using `pre*` lifecycle hooks rather than extending `"build"` is deliberate: `npm run build` is
already a `&&` chain, and CLAUDE.md records that chain short-circuiting on a Prettier failure and
silently leaving a stale `dist` — a trap TR-076, TR-077 and TR-078 each hit. Adding a fourth link
would have widened it.

### Both branches proven by execution

- **Source-absent (CI's state):** run from a working directory where the relative source path
  cannot resolve — prints `source pack absent … assets are committed, nothing to rebuild`,
  verifies the 16 bodies / 86 files on disk, **exits 0**.
- **Drift detection:** removing `dione-normal-high.jpg` and re-running `--verify` **exits 1** with
  `dione: declared output dione-normal-high.jpg is missing`. A gate that has never failed proves
  nothing; this one was made to fail on purpose.

### `NEVER_SPHERE` is duplicated, so parity is asserted

The owner's standing rule (Phobos and Deimos stay billboards permanently — the shape _is_ the
science) is enforced in two places that cannot import each other: a `.mjs` build script decides
what gets a texture, and `planet-sphere.ts` decides what gets a sphere. Since the pipeline is
data-driven — a body becomes spherical the moment the manifest contains it — a divergence would
silently sphere a potato. `tests/unit/planet-asset-pipeline.test.ts` asserts the two sets are
identical, applying CLAUDE.md #4's twin discipline to data rather than to shaders.

## Part 5 — The asset budget, and it caught its own author

The owner's third deviation: `budget:check` gated JS and WASM only, while `public/assets` grew to
209 MB across PF-10 with nothing watching it. See **[ADR-0009](../adr/0009-asset-weight-budget.md)**
for the decision; the measurement notes follow.

Every budget — including the JS/WASM ones — moved out of the measurement script into
`budgets.config.mjs` as data, so tuning a ceiling is a one-line change in a file with no logic in
it. Assets are gated in **raw** bytes (JPEG/PNG are already entropy-coded; gzipping ~2,600 files
would burn CPU to find them ~0% smaller) and read from `public/assets` rather than `dist/`, which
is what makes the check build-free and therefore usable from the dev-time hooks.

**The gate failed on its first real run, against this session's own work**, which is the outcome
that demonstrates it rather than merely describing it:

```
 FAIL  assets total (raw): 255.26 MB / budget 209.00 MB
 FAIL    assets/planets: 168.23 MB / budget 122.00 MB
```

Raised deliberately to 255.5 / 168.5 with the cause recorded in the same file. Per-directory
attribution is what made that a one-line diagnosis rather than an investigation.

### Two findings the gate surfaced immediately

**6.58 MB of `-base` (2048) tier maps are shipped but never fetched.** `babylon-engine.ts` reads
only `.high` and `.ultra`; the `base` tier, described in the pipeline as "what every device gets",
was never wired to anything. Kept rather than trimmed, because C4.3's tier gating is precisely the
work that will consume it and deleting it now means rebaking it then — but it is now a recorded
quantity rather than an undiscovered one.

**Current `assets/planets` breakdown**, so a future trim has somewhere to start:

## Part 6 — I deleted 19 real assets, and the E2E caught it

This is the most important part of this report, and it is a defect I introduced while building the
tooling meant to prevent exactly this class of problem.

The pipeline's first `pruneOrphans()` removed every `.jpg` in `public/assets/planets` that no
declared body produced. That directory is **shared**. It also held:

- the **18 per-body dossier images** the celestial catalogs reference (`mars.jpg`, `moon-topo.jpg`,
  `venus.jpg`, …), consumed via `img:` fields in `src/data/celestial/*.js`;
- **`venus-cloud.jpg`**, hand-placed by TR-078's Venus descent, **untracked by git** and so not
  recoverable from it.

All 19 were deleted. Nothing failed at build, typecheck, lint or unit test. It surfaced as
`404 /assets/planets/mars.jpg` in a **real-browser E2E**, and only because those specs assert
console-cleanliness rather than mere readiness — TR-045's discipline catching a defect from a
direction it was never designed for.

**Recovery:** the 18 catalog images were git-tracked and restored with `git checkout`.
`venus-cloud.jpg` was re-copied from its source pack, byte-identical (93,299 bytes), verified at its
documented 1024×512.

**The general rule, worth more than the fix:** a destructive step must be scoped by what it
**owns**, never by what it **expects**. "I did not expect this file" and "this file is mine to
delete" are different claims, and only the second licenses an `rm`. Both the pruner and
`--verify`'s orphan check are now scoped by a `GENERATED_NAME` pattern the pipeline can actually
produce; anything unrecognised is reported and left alone.

**Two things this exposed that were true before I touched anything:**

1. **`venus-cloud.jpg` was never in any pipeline** — hand-copied from a _different_ source pack
   (`gaia_datasets/default-data`, not `hi-res-textures`), which is why this session's reckoning
   never saw it. That is precisely the regeneration hazard the owner asked to close, in a file the
   deviation report did not know about. It is now a declared `PASSTHROUGH_ASSETS` entry: protected,
   verified, reproducible.
2. **A reckoning covers the pack it enumerates, which is not the same as covering the assets.** The
   fix for deviation #1 was sound and still missed this, because it asked "is every file in the
   pack accounted for?" rather than "is every shipped asset accounted for?"

**Three new tests** pin it: the pruner's ownership pattern (with `venus-cloud.jpg` as an explicit
negative case), the passthrough declaration, and — the one that would have caught it in seconds — a
check that **every `assets/planets/*.jpg` referenced by the celestial catalogs exists on disk**,
derived from the catalogs themselves rather than from a second list that could drift.

**It also invalidated a measurement.** The asset budget's 254.5 MB "high-water mark" was taken while
those 19 files were missing; restoring them added 1.77 MB and the gate failed again. Corrected to
256.5 with the reason recorded in `budgets.config.mjs` — a measurement is only as trustworthy as
the state it was taken in.

## Part 7 — Earth, after Astra's brief landed

This report's first draft recorded Earth's night lights, clouds and ocean specular as
**deliberately not implemented**, because the Astra brief commissioned for them had not returned
and guessing would have been the Venus mistake. The brief then landed mid-session
([earth-sphere-science-brief](../analysis/2026-07-21-earth-sphere-science-brief.md), 1,572 lines).
The stop was right; this is what it bought.

### The finding that reframes the whole C4 phase, not just Earth

**The arrival phase angle is exactly 0.000°, for every body, every time.** Measured out of the
engine rather than assumed: `travelTo` parks the camera at `bodyPos − dir·ARRIVE_STANDOFF` — on the
Sun–body line — and `sunDirectionFrom` returns `−dir`, so **V = L** identically. Free-look changes
orientation only.

| Quantity                          | At the parked camera                     |
| --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| N·L across the framed 23.5° patch | **1.000 → 0.979** (2.1% variation)       |
| Terminator                        | **never in frame** (90° from sub-camera) |
| Night hemisphere                  | **100% occluded**                        |
| Specular mirror point             | **exactly at frame centre**              |

This **additively corrects the C4.1 brief**, which named "real phases and a real terminator" as
C4's honest headline. Correct about the physics of a sphere; false about this scene's arrival
geometry. **No body in this scene ever shows a phase.** The earlier claim stands in its own
document with the correction recorded there.

### What shipped

| Element            | Verdict                     | Shipped                                                                           |
| ------------------ | --------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Night lights**   | **BROKEN PHYSICS if shown** | **Not shipped; assets deleted.** See below                                        |
| Surface albedo     | BROKEN PHYSICS at 0.434     | **0.213** clear-sky — 0.434 is a two-layer composite and double-counts the clouds |
| Lunar-Lambert L    | BROKEN PHYSICS at 0.55/1.0  | **0** — a regolith shadow-hiding law does not describe an ocean                   |
| Ocean glint        | **ACCURATE and mandatory**  | Cox-Munk σ² = 0.03884 at the real 7.0 m/s mean wind; Fresnel F₀ = 0.02101         |
| Rayleigh + aerosol | **BROKEN PHYSICS to omit**  | τ = (0.0491, 0.0973, 0.2211), real λ⁻⁴·⁰⁹ colour and phase function               |
| Cloud deck         | ACCURATE                    | Raw alpha, white cloud, L = 0.9, **locked to surface rotation**                   |
| Blue limb ring     | omitting is honest          | Not drawn — the limb is 43.155° off-axis against a 22.92° half-FOV                |

**The night lights are the decision worth recording.** They are the most beautiful asset in the
pack, they are real NASA data, and rendering them would have looked wonderful — exactly the profile
of the Venus radar-lighting error. At zero phase the night hemisphere is entirely behind the
planet; no exposure, threshold or mask puts a city light on that screen. The only ways to show them
are to lie about the Sun direction, lie about N·L, or composite the map unconditionally. Astra
ranks it **broken physics #1**: the map is real, the _view_ would be invented. The three baked files
were removed and the reason recorded in `PACK_RECKONING`, so it is a revisitable decision — the
moment the arrival geometry changes they become one of the best things available in this scene.

**Two corrections Astra made to constants I had already entered**, both found by checking a number
against its own stated source:

- `dione` rotation was **236,429 s** against the 2.736915 d its own comment cited — a 40.5 s slip.
  Now 236,469.5.
- Dione, Rhea and Tethys were falling back to `lunarLambertL = 0.55`, which is **Mars's**
  coefficient, and Mars has an atmosphere. Airless icy regolith takes 1.0, like the Moon.

### The brightness conflict: my diagnosis was wrong, and the real finding is bigger

This report's first draft flagged that Tethys's geometric albedo of 1.229 would reach ~8.8× the
surface value and blow out. **Astra measured it and that is wrong**: at zero phase V = L, so μ ≡ μ₀
for _any_ normal and `refl` is **exactly 1.000** at L = 1. Against the real shipped normal maps the
worst in-frame value is **1.24**.

**The real problem is `albedo × 3.6 × texture`, and it is systemic.** Measured mean output at
`refl = 1`: Dione 2.17, Europa 2.04, Tethys 1.80, Rhea 1.30, Venus 1.25, Io 1.21 — **all clipped**.
Only Mars (0.25) and the Moon (0.28), the two bodies 3.6 was tuned against, fit. Texture means span
0.380–0.844, a 2.2× exposure spread multiplying straight in, because C4.1's intended
bake-normalisation was never implemented.

There is also physics hidden in the albedos: geometric albedo is defined at α = 0.000°, and local
phase rises at 0.045°/px, so the opposition surge is a **2–11 px** dot (CBOE) or a **22–134 px**
spot (SHOE) on a 1198-px disc. Applying p = 1.229 uniformly paints a two-pixel backscatter spike
across the whole body — the same shape of error as lighting a radar map.

**Deliberately not fixed here**, and flagged rather than half-done: the correct fix moves the surge
out of `uAlbedo` into `refl` (`surge = 1 + B₀·exp(−α/2°)`, base = Bond albedo), then chooses between
bake-normalisation (exact ratios, but the Moon goes 2.6× darker) and a Reinhard response curve
(never clips, compresses the real 9.0:1 Tethys:Moon ratio to 2.9:1). That is an exposure-pipeline
change touching every body, it needs the owner's call between two honest options, and it is not a
rider on a deviation closeout. Astra's numbers for both routes are in the brief.

**One consequence worth stating plainly:** at zero phase with L = 1 the nine new normal maps
contribute **0% at the frame centre** and at most ±16% at the edge. Not a defect in the maps — it is
the full-Moon effect arriving exactly as the physics predicts — but their visible payoff is gated on
the same arrival-geometry decision as everything else above.

**Also outstanding, unchanged from TR-078 Part 7:** the ADR-0008 real-device pass, the belt's 23.44°
frame decision, and C4.3.

## Part 8 — superseded draft text (kept per the corrections-are-additive convention)

The following was this report's Part 6 before Astra's brief landed. It is left standing because it
records a decision that was correct when made — stopping rather than guessing — and because the

> **Earth ships as a real sphere, but only half the Earth feature exists.**

This is deliberate, and it is the Venus lesson applied rather than ignored. TR-078 Part 2 recorded

to make impressive and easy to make wrong (city lights as a glowing decal, a cloud shell at a scale
that cannot be represented, a specular highlight at a phase angle where none would be visible).

An Astra science brief was commissioned for exactly these questions and had not returned when this
report was written. Implementing ahead of it would have been guessing with a good result and no
justification. **The state on disk is coherent and correct** — a real Earth, correctly lit, with
real relief — so nothing ships wrong; it ships incomplete, which is stated here rather than
smoothed over.

**Also outstanding, unchanged from TR-078 Part 7:** the ADR-0008 real-device pass, the belt's
23.44° frame decision, and C4.3.

**One provisional set of constants** is flagged rather than presented as settled. Dione, Rhea and
Tethys carry geometric albedos of 0.998 / 0.949 / **1.229** — values that genuinely exceed 1
(E-ring resurfacing plus a strong coherent-backscatter opposition surge; Verbiscer et al. 2007) and
must not be "fixed" by clamping, since being conspicuously brighter than the Moon beside them is
the most distinctive true fact about these bodies. But the shipped reflectance is
`surface * albedo * (refl * dayside * 3.6 + 0.06)`, where 3.6 was tuned when the brightest body was
Venus at 0.689. Tethys can reach ~8.8× the surface value and blow out to white — losing exactly the
detail the new normal maps were added to reveal. This is a real realism-vs-tuning conflict, it is
referred to Astra for quantification of **both** sides, and it is recorded here rather than
silently resolved in either direction.

### The defect: four bodies ship textures but cannot be reached

`travelTo("earth")` and `travelTo("tethys")` do nothing. Checking the catalogs explains why:

| Body                       | In `src/data/celestial/` | Assets shipped |
| -------------------------- | ------------------------ | -------------- |
| earth, tethys, dione, rhea | **absent**               | **28.35 MB**   |

This is **this session's own recurring mistake, one level up**. Part 1 records that "the search was

live and reachable on six bodies (io, europa, titan, pluto, ganymede, ceres); the other three

**Gated, not just noted.** `tests/unit/planet-asset-pipeline.test.ts` now asserts every sphered
body is either in the catalog or in an explicit `UNREACHABLE_BY_DESIGN` record with a reason — and
a second test fails if a name stays in that record after its catalog entry lands, so the deviation
cannot go stale in either direction. **Resolution is an owner decision** (add catalog entries with
real positions, or drop 28.35 MB of assets) and is deliberately not taken here.

## Part 8c — The pruner's delete capability is removed entirely (owner direction)

Part 6 narrowed the orphan-pruner to filenames matching `GENERATED_NAME`, which would have been
safe. The owner removed the capability anyway, and the reason is better than safety:

> The approved design said **"no orphans — fails loud on drift"**. "Fails loud" is a report, not an
> `rm`. The destructive step was never requested.

It also solved a problem that has occurred zero times (a body dropped from the pipeline leaving
megabytes behind) at the cost of a failure mode that destroys work — and did, twice, in one
session.

`pruneOrphans()` is now `reportUnrecognised()`. It classifies what it finds into two buckets and
prints them; it deletes nothing. `rmSync` is gone from the file, and a unit test asserts the
**absence of any delete call** (`rmSync`, `unlinkSync`, `rmdirSync`, `rm(`) rather than asserting
that deletion is careful — "cannot delete" is a strictly stronger claim than "deletes correctly",
and it is the one worth pinning.

`--verify` still fails on a true orphan, so drift is still caught. It is now caught by a person.

**The rule this leaves in the codebase:** the pipeline writes what it declares and reports what it
does not recognise. **Removing a file is a human decision.**

measured things.**
