# TR-037 — Babylon Sky ~6× Denser Than the Live Engine (procedural placeholder, not a defect in B2 step 1)

**Date:** 2026-07-19
**Scope:** Owner observation on physical Android: _"the sky has 6× density of stars, which was the
same even after rebuild for babylon engine. Which is different to the original webgl engine."_
**Verdict: OVERALL: READY TO PROCEED** — the observation is correct, the cause is the known
procedural placeholder, and it is exactly what B2 step 2 replaces. No regression from B2 step 1.

## The observation is right, and it is not caused by the vertex expansion

Two distinct claims were in the owner's report and I initially collapsed them into one:

| Claim                                         | Bears on              | Status                         |
| --------------------------------------------- | --------------------- | ------------------------------ |
| Sky **unchanged before vs after** the rebuild | B2 step 1 correctness | ✅ pass (transparent refactor) |
| Sky **~6× denser than the WebGL engine**      | Scene fidelity        | ⚠️ real gap — this report      |

[TR-036](TR-036.md) recorded the first and wrote as though it settled the second. It does not.
Corrected there.

## Root cause — uniform placeholder brightness vs real photometry

Both engines draw the **same 168,959 sprites**. Verified, no duplication:
`activeIndices 1,013,754 ÷ 6 = 168,959` quads. The difference is entirely in how many of them are
_visible_.

**Live engine** ([space-engine.js](../../src/lib/space-engine.js), star vertex shader) is
photometric:

```glsl
float mag  = 12.5 - aMeta.x*14.0;            // real catalog magnitude
float flux = pow(10.0, -0.4*(mag - 2.0));    // Pogson's law
px     = clamp((0.9 + 2.6*pow(flux,0.28)) * uSize * (520.0/dist), 1.0, 13.0);
vAlpha = 0.10 + 0.90*sqrt(clamp(flux, 0.0, 1.4));
```

The magnitude distribution of a real catalog is steeply weighted toward faint objects, so the
**large majority of those 168,959 stars land at ~1 px with alpha ≈ 0.10** — present but
essentially invisible. The eye resolves a few thousand.

**Babylon** ([star-field.ts](../../src/lib/star-field.ts) + its shader twins) is a placeholder:

```ts
meta[i * 2] = 0.3 + rnd() * 0.7; // uniform random size — no magnitude model at all
```

```glsl
float a = exp(-d*d*4.0);         // every star gets a full-brightness core
```

Every star is uniformly sized and fully bright, so **all 168,959 are visible**. Same count, an
order of magnitude more perceived stars. "~6× denser" is a reasonable eyeball estimate of that.

Compounding it: the procedural positions are **uniform on a sphere shell**, whereas the real sky
concentrates along the Milky Way band and leaves large sparse regions. Uniform noise reads as
denser than a structured sky even at equal count.

## This was known and recorded — but as a caveat, not as a visible symptom

The [gate procedure](../validation-checklist/2026-07-18-pf09-b1-gate-measurement.md) already noted
the spike renders "a **representative** 168k-star field (procedural), not the full catalog +
photometric/relativistic shaders". What was not written down anywhere is what that _looks like_ —
so the first person to view it on a real screen reasonably read it as a bug.

**Worth carrying forward:** when a placeholder is visually obvious, say so in the artefact that
someone will read _before_ they look at the screen. "Procedural, not photometric" is accurate and
still fails to convey "the sky will look several times too dense".

## Consequence for the B1 fps figures — they are pessimistic, not optimistic

Previously recorded (TR-035, gate doc) as an **upper bound** because the spike lacked the catalog
and shaders. On the fill-rate dimension specifically, the opposite holds: the placeholder draws
**every** star at full size and brightness, where the real photometric path collapses most of them
to 1 px at alpha 0.10. Babylon's 60 fps on Android was therefore achieved while **overdrawing
relative to the real scene**.

Net: the startup figure is still an upper bound (no catalog fetch/decode, no ship), but the fps
figure carries genuine headroom that photometric sizing should widen rather than consume.

## Fix — B2 step 2, unchanged in scope

Replacing `buildStarField`'s seeded-LCG placeholder with the real Gaia/Hipparcos catalog and
porting the photometric mapping (`mag → flux → size/alpha`) fixes density and appearance together;
they are the same defect. No new work item — this is the step already sequenced next.

Explicitly **not** doing a cosmetic patch (e.g. lowering `uScale` or dimming the ramp) to make the
placeholder look sparser. That would tune a fake distribution toward a real one by eye, then have
to be untuned when the catalog lands.
