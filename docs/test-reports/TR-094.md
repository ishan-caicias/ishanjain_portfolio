## What this slice is

The second half of owner complaint R6 ("...and abrupt flip"). The flip occupied 84–252 ms — below
the ~1 s threshold at which a 180° rotation reads as a manoeuvre rather than a cut — and on any
frame slower than ~195 ms the phase was skipped entirely (TR-081), which is both a visitor who
never sees a flip and the GAP-17 `indexOf("flip") === -1` flake. D3.2 widens the window, floors its
screen time, and spends it on a five-beat manoeuvre.

## The design-pass finding (why this slice did not follow its own plan step)

The implementation plan's prescribed floor — _"if `0.12 × warpDur < FLIP_MIN_MS`, stretch
`warpDur` to `FLIP_MIN_MS / 0.12`; the ≈12.5 s floor never triggers"_ — is **arithmetically
backwards**. `warpDurationForLy` spans 1,400–4,200 ms, so `0.12 × warpDur` spans 168–504 ms and is
**always** below 1,500 ms: the branch fires on every journey and sets every warp to 12.5 s (a 3–9×
inflation). Its companion claim, "home stays snappy via its own `WARP_MIN_MS`", is false under any
correct fixed floor. Both are corrected in [ADR-0011](../adr/0011-warp-velocity-profile-v4.md) and
superseded with a dated note in the implementation plan. This is the D0.1 lesson applied — _probe a
prescribed mechanism before implementing on top of it_.

## What shipped

1. **v4 trapezoid profile** (`warpEaseV4`, ship-dynamics.ts) — quadratic burn, **genuine
   constant-velocity coast** across `[WARP_ACCEL_END, WARP_DECEL_START]`, quadratic brake. This is
   Astra §3.2's choreography expressed in the curve: engines cut → coast while rotating → relight.
   The old triangle could not represent it, so every speed cue _sagged_ through exactly the window
   where the narrative says "coasting".
2. **Screen-time floor via an in-window rate multiplier.** `warpFlipRate(dur)` returns
   `r = min(1, 0.12·dur/1500)`; k advances at `slow × r` inside the window, so the window lasts
   `max(0.12·dur, 1500 ms)` exactly. The coast slope is `m/r`, so **ds/dk rises by 1/r precisely
   where dk/dt falls by r — world velocity is continuous at both edges** (unit-asserted). The naive
   alternative lurches ~4× at polaris; ADR-0011 rejected it explicitly.
3. **`warpSpeedNorm` replaces the inline `dsdk` triangle** as the single cue driver. Aberration β,
   the D3.1 FOV breath and the HUD `vC` now **hold peak through the coast** instead of sagging.
4. **Window widened** `0.47/0.53 → 0.44/0.56`. `wphase` already sourced these constants (D3.1's
   one-place-change setup), so the HUD label boundaries moved automatically.
5. **Five-beat choreography** (Vega): burn cutoff (eased `burnEnvelope`, ~150 ms) → drift beat →
   **C² smootherstep rotation** across `[FLIP_ROT_START 0.465, FLIP_ROT_END 0.545]` → settle beat →
   retro relight (~0.3 s shoulder, capped so short hops compress rather than eat the brake). The
   `env` scalar is threaded through `PlumeParams` **additively** — absent means the original boolean
   behaviour, so every non-warp caller and existing plume expectation is untouched.
6. **RCS puff couples** at rotation start and end, reusing the ember buffer (no new mesh, material
   or asset): opposed lateral bursts at the two hull ends — the couple that actually produces a
   pitch rotation. Skipped under reduced motion.
7. **Hidden fourth threshold consumer, found by the blast-radius sweep:**
   `NEBULA_REVEAL.decelStart` was a `0.53` literal in nebula-field.ts — re-keyed to `0.56`, else the
   destination gas begins revealing while the ship is still mid-rotation.
8. **`sceneStats()` gains `flipRate` + `burnEnv`** so the floor and the drive envelope are
   machine-checkable.

### The belt-slowdown regression: root cause and the honest fix

The v4 profile broke the `warpSlowMin < 0.9` assertion on the m42 route — **deterministically**
(isolation ×3: 0.948 / 0.948 / 0.978), so not the rotating-flake class. The investigation, in
order, because each step killed the previous hypothesis:

1. **Load-sparsity hypothesis — rejected by isolation.** 3/3 deterministic failures meant
   something systematic.
2. **Pure-math simulation with the REAL functions** (scratch vitest sim, deleted after use):
   v4 reaches warpSlowMin ≈ 0.4623 at 60/15/5 fps — matching the true line minimum (0.4623 at
   e=0.326). The profile math was exonerated; the divergence had to be engine-side.
3. **In-browser trajectory probe** (scratch spec, deleted after use): the whole 4.66 s m42
   journey rendered in **10 frames — ~2 fps under SwiftShader** — and the two samples around the
   belt sat at e=0.169 and e=0.442, stepping ~140 world units clean over the tube (core at
   e=0.326, σ=32).

**The real finding: `warpSlowMin` was always a point-sampled instrument, and its own spec
comment ("captured engine-side … no timing sensitivity") was never true.** The old triangle
profile passed at 2 fps by **geometric accident** — its k≈0.35 sample happened to land on the
tube edge (slow ≈ 0.83, just under the 0.9 threshold). v4's slightly gentler burn (the coast is
funded by reducing `m`) moved that sample earlier on the path and the accident stopped covering
for the latent fragility. A pre-existing defect exposed, not created, by this slice.

**Fix (real, not test surgery):** new pure `minSlowAlongSegment` (babylon-asteroids.ts) —
the minimum slow factor along the SEGMENT a frame traverses, substepped at σ/2 (capped 24
substeps; the tube is ~±3σ wide so even the capped-coarse pass cannot miss the core).
`_warpSlowMin` now records the segment minimum; the per-frame point sample still drives the
feedback (flight feel), which is unchanged on real hardware where frames step ~3 wu. The record
now means what it claims — **the same journey reports the same minimum at 2 fps as at 60** —
which is the repo's own "deterministic-feeling under variable frame times" doctrine applied to
an instrument. No E2E assertion was touched; the spec passes 3/3 in isolation post-fix.

**Test-instrument lesson (cost two red runs, recorded because the failures were MINE, not the
code's).** Two of the new assertions were wrong instruments:

- **World-velocity continuity** probed the slope at `KA ± 1e-3`. The burn slope rises _linearly_, so
  a probe 1e-3 before the edge reads ~0.23% low — the algebraic property is exact, the probe wasn't.
  Replaced with an algebraic recovery of `m` from a mid-segment slope (where the segment is smooth),
  asserting `coastSlope · r === m`.
- **C² rotation** used a nested finite difference _at_ the clamp join; that stencil straddles the
  piecewise boundary and reports the curve's cubic growth as a ~3.9 second-derivative spike.
  Replaced with the smootherstep signature `f/t³ → 10` (vs smoothstep's `3/t` ≈ 300 at t=0.01) — a
  30× discriminator that is numerically stable. Also worth recording: `10t³` is only the **leading
  term**; comparing to it at 9-decimal precision fails against the exact `10t³ − 15t⁴ + 6t⁵`.

**eslint config fix (unblocked the build chain).** `playwright-report/` and `test-results/` are
declared generated in `.gitignore` but were missing from the eslint ignores, so a local E2E run
with the default `html` reporter dropped **~2,900 lint errors** of Playwright's own bundled
trace-viewer assets into the tree — and since `npm run build` is `lint && check && astro build`,
that **blocked the build**. Added to `eslint.config.js` with the reason inline. Latent since the
html reporter was configured; surfaced by this slice's test runs.

**What automated coverage proves:** the floor arithmetic is exact for every shipped duration; the
coast is genuinely flat (slope constant across the window); world velocity is continuous at both
edges; `warpSpeedNorm` holds 1 across the window; the rotation is C² and sits strictly inside the
window with drift/settle beats intact; the burn envelope cuts across the whole rotation and ramps
back monotonically. **What needs owner eyes** (SHOT-BRIEF §7): whether the flip reads as three
distinct events, whether the RCS puffs read as cold gas rather than fire, that the sky does _not_
slow during the coast, and the home-journey pacing question.

## REVIEW addendum (2026-07-24, same session — Fable 5, post-gate adversarial pass over D3.1 + D3.2)

The owner-requested review of both slices found **one real defect** (fixed same turn) and
confirmed the rest of the diff clean:

**DEFECT — the floor had a single-frame hole, and three documents were claiming it didn't.**
The in-window rate was selected once from k at frame start, so a frame beginning just below
`WARP_ACCEL_END` advanced its entire step at rate 1 — and at the `_dtWarpS` cap (0.5 s),
`dk ≈ 0.13 > 0.12`: **one capped frame could still jump the whole flip window**, the exact
TR-081 skip class ADR-0011, the delivery plan note and this TR asserted dead. GAP-17's history
proves ~0.5 s frames occur under load, so the hole was reachable. Fixed with `advanceWarpV4`
(ship-dynamics.ts): a piecewise integrator that splits the step at the window boundaries and
spends the post-boundary remainder at rate `r` — time-exact under any dt sequence. Three unit
tests pin it: the worst-case capped frame lands INSIDE the window (can never exit it in one
step); the window consumes exactly `max(0.12·dur, FLIP_MIN_MS)` of wall-clock under a
deliberately awkward mixed-dt integration; and `r = 1` degenerates to the plain advance.
`advanceWarpProgress` itself is untouched (still unit-pinned; no other engine caller).

**Checked and cleared:** RCS `Math.random()` use matches the existing ember-spawn convention
(cosmetic one-shots, not seeded field construction); `_rcsFired` latches reset per journey and
are reduced-motion-exempt; `_burnEnv`/`_relightK` staleness is benign (last value ≈ 1 at
arrival); the relight shoulder cap arithmetic is correct at both duration extremes; the
`warpSlowMin` segment fix guards reduced motion and uses consistent ship-offset endpoints; the
HUD `vC` keeps its historical nominal calibration (peak value preserved; the plateau is the new
honest part — absolute calibration is Astra's post-D3 audit item); chase/fade/nebula threshold
consumers all verified against the widened window.

**Gate sequencing recorded honestly:** run 2 (belt fix) completed on the pre-review build;
run 3 below is the binding gate on the final code including `advanceWarpV4`.

**Second finding closed during the gate — frame-ladder's arrival budgets (named #15 change).**
Run 3's single failure (frame-ladder goHome poll) reproduced 4/4 in isolation at the _polaris_
leg — a rotating failure POINT, which ruled out a deterministic engine defect, and a minimal
sparse-polling probe of the same mars→polaris journey arrived cleanly in 4 s with zero console
errors. Root cause: under SwiftShader, the spec's own ~100 ms `sceneStats` polling starves rAF
(TR-089 measured multi-second evals) so frames land ~every 2 s while `_dtWarpS` caps each at
0.5 s — **wall-clock ≈ 4× engine time**. ADR-0011's floor lengthens every journey ~1.1–1.3 s by
design; at 4× that is +4–5 s of wall-clock, which pushed the spec's 20 s arrival polls over
their margin (the same near-zero-margin ceiling class TR-080/081 recalibrated). Fix: arrival
polls 20 s → 40 s and total 180 s → 240 s in `frame-ladder.spec.ts` — time budgets only, no
assertion or expected value touched, reason documented at the change. 3/3 green in isolation
after. Watch item: `engine-select`'s 8 s arrival budgets have less margin now too — green across
all runs, but the first place to look if that file starts rotating.
