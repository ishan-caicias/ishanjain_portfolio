## What this slice is

`PLANET_LUNAR_L.earth = 0` and `PLANET_ALBEDO.earth = 0.213` were fitted at α = 0, and two shipped
features (the D6.4 goHome reveal, the D1.3 launch ascent) render Earth at α = 90.0000°. The D6.4
realism review flagged it; the D1.3 review **escalated** it — "should block the next Earth-visual
change", with two concrete asks: (1) the surface's effective Lunar-Lambert coefficient at 90° for
a water/land composite, and (2) a re-check of the 0.213 clear-sky split at half-terminator
geometry. This slice answers both with computed numbers and ships the resulting one-constant fix.

## What Astra found (full derivation: Earth brief Addendum 3)

**The measured target.** Mallama et al. (2017)'s Earth phase polynomial — fitted to EPOXI
spectrophotometry taken **near quadrature**, exactly this scene's geometry — gives
Δm(90°) = 1.568 mag → **Φ_E(90°) = 0.236**.

**The shipped shader, disc-integrated** (400k-sample Fibonacci-sphere integration of the actual
fragment-shader formulas, instrument validated by reproducing the analytic Lambert
Φ(90°) = 0.3183 exactly — `scripts/solve-earth-quadrature.mjs`):

| Quantity                       | Value                     |
| ------------------------------ | ------------------------- |
| Shipped composite Φ(90°)       | **0.354**                 |
| Measured Φ_E(90°)              | **0.236**                 |
| Over-brightness at quadrature  | **×1.50 (+0.44 mag)**     |
| Surface (Lambert) component    | 0.3183                    |
| Cloud (L = 0.9 slab) component | **0.3727 — the offender** |

**Verdicts on the two asks:** (1) surface **L = 0 CONFIRMED at all phases** — the no-regolith
mechanism is phase-independent; (2) **0.213 CONFIRMED** — its α = 0 anchor was independently
corroborated and is not the deficit's source. The real gap: Chandrasekhar's conservative-slab
form has no forward-scattering deficit, but real Mie droplets (g ≈ 0.85) scatter strongly
forward — a real cloud deck at 90° scattering angle is far darker than the slab predicts, which
is the dominant reason Earth's measured phase curve is so much steeper than Lambert.

## What shipped

One solved constant (the `EXPOSURE_GAIN` method — solved by bisection on the disc integral, not
tuned), applied to the cloud reflectance in **both shader twins**, line-parallel:

```
cloudRefl *= 1 − CLOUD_QUAD_DEFICIT · min(alphaDeg / 90, 1),   CLOUD_QUAD_DEFICIT = 0.539
```

- `Φ_shader(90°) = 0.2360` — lands on the measured curve exactly.
- The factor is **exactly 1 at α = 0**, so every `travelTo` arrival (all at 0.000°) and Venus are
  **bit-identical** — only the goHome reveal and the launch ascent change.
- The `min()` clamp stops extrapolation past 90°, where real crescent Earth shows forward-scatter
  _excess_ this form cannot represent (the scene never renders that geometry).
- **Ledger L18** (Earth brief Addendum 3): the linear-in-α interpolation between the two
  calibrated endpoints is a declared license — the real curve between is convex; the scene renders
  only the endpoints, which are measured-exact.

**Frontier note, date-stamped:** a July 2025 paper (PSJ, arXiv:2507.22258) revises Earth's visual
geometric albedo to 0.242 ± 0.005 vs Mallama 2017's 0.434 — a live dispute about the _full-phase
extrapolation_. This fix is a **ratio** calibration and both analyses fit the same
quadrature-region data, so it is robust to the dispute; watch-listed for any future
absolute-albedo rebase.
